## 1. Overview

Delta Horizon is a remote sea island unlocked in the postgame. It serves as the game's Battle Frontier equivalent — a dedicated destination for high-level competitive play, built around the Delta phenomenon and inhabited by a community of strong trainers who arrived independently and chose to stay.

The island is inaccessible during the main game. Once unlocked, it becomes a Fly destination on the regional map.

The name echoes the TCG's own descriptor for Dragon Frontiers — "beyond the horizon" — placing Delta Horizon as the region's furthest point, beyond even Dragon Frontiers.

## 2. Access & Unlock

**Unlock condition:** To be decided. Candidates:

- The sea route is too dangerous while Rayquaza δ is actively territorial; clears naturally when the main story resolves
- The island community does not accept visitors until the player becomes Champion
- The boat captain who knows the route was waiting to see how the Delta crisis resolved before making the trip

**Travel method:** Boat from a coastal port on first visit, then Fly on return visits.

## 3. The Island

### 3.1 Identity

A remote island that was too far or too dangerous to reach during the main game. Terrain is varied — beaches, interior jungle or highland, some elevation. Delta Pokémon here are stable and powerful, produced by long-term natural Delta rather than Tower broadcast. The island has its own distinct biome, to be defined.

### 3.2 The Community

A self-contained community of trainers who sought out the Delta phenomenon and chose to remain. They arrived independently — wanderers, researchers who left the faction, travelers who followed the outward pulse — and formed an informal settlement around shared respect for the Delta ecosystem. They are not affiliated with the Holon research team and operate entirely outside that world.

The community gives the island life beyond the battle facilities. A handful of NPCs, small dwellings, and a port connecting back to the mainland make it feel inhabited rather than purely functional.

## 4. Facilities

Four facilities, each a distinct battle format. All named in the Delta convention.

### 4.1 Delta Colosseum

**Emerald equivalent:** Battle Tower

Standard ranked battles. Win streaks earn BP. Singles and doubles formats. The competitive centerpiece of the island — straightforward in structure, high in difficulty. The Colosseum itself is built into whatever ancient stonework or natural formation defines the island's most prominent feature.

### 4.2 Delta Preserve

**Emerald equivalent:** Battle Factory

Rental battles using a pool composed entirely of Delta Species Pokémon. The player builds a team from available rentals and can swap after wins. Thematically clean — the Preserve name implies the island's Delta Pokémon are documented and available for study through battle, not just observation.

### 4.3 Delta Labyrinth

**Emerald equivalent:** Battle Pyramid

A dungeon crawl through the island's interior or underground. Floors increase in difficulty, items are restricted, and information degrades with each level. The crystal cave aesthetic of the wider region fits naturally — the Labyrinth runs through crystal-embedded rock formations, with Holon Circle-style crystal clusters marking key chambers.

### 4.4 Delta Passage

**Emerald equivalent:** Battle Pike

A linear route through the island's terrain divided into rooms with random outcomes — ambush trainers, healing, item finds, or nothing. Shorter runs than the Labyrinth but less predictable. The Passage structure suits a coastal or jungle route threading through the island's natural geography.
