*Document ID: HL-104*

This document serves as the definitive, unified canon for the Holon region, synthesizing the overarching narrative history, the progression of the Delta Species mutation, and the deep environmental storytelling extracted from the background art of the five canonical TCG sets.

# PART I: The Synthesized Lore Guide

## 1. The Core Narrative: The Search for Mew

### The Original Expedition
The Holon story does not begin with the Delta Species, but rather with a scientific expedition into a pristine, primeval wilderness known as the **Mirage Forest**. A specialized team of researchers, scientists, and fieldworkers journeyed here with a singular goal: to locate and study the elusive Mythical Pokémon, **Mew**. 

Their research identified a location deep within the forest known as the **Power Spot**—an ancient, glowing clearing brimming with a mysterious healing energy. They chose to build their research settlement directly on top of this spot to study its effects, while simultaneously preparing to track Mew.

### The Delta Incident
To study the Power Spot and construct a radar array powerful enough to track Mew, the researchers built the **Holon Research Tower** directly over the site. The tower was a colossal, brutalist metal spire that fired intense electromagnetic tracking waves into the atmosphere.

While the tower functioned as designed, it had severe, unintended consequences. The intense EM radiation bombarded the local wildlife, artificially mutating the Pokémon into the **δ Delta Species**. In the immediate vicinity of the tower, fully evolved δ Delta Species Pokémon almost always gained a secondary **Metal-type**, a trait directly attributed to the tower's powerful EM radiation.

The scope of the TCG expansions gradually expands outward from the tower, tracking the presence of Delta Pokémon across increasingly wild and remote regions: from the submerged machinery of Holon Lake, across the ocean to the Crystal Island, and finally to the edge of the world: The Dragon Frontiers.

## 2. Geographical & Environmental Masterclass

The Holon region is characterized by extreme dualism: pristine, ancient wilderness violently contrasting with high-tech, brutalist scientific intrusion and ancient forgotten ruins. 

### 2.1 The Primeval Mirage Forest (Ground Zero)
The forest is an incredibly dense, Jurassic-like jungle characterized by massive trees and hanging vines. It is bathed in a perpetual, thick mist (the "Mirage"). 
*   **The Power Spots:** Small, isolated clearings bathed in ethereal pink or yellow dappled sunlight where Mew and Ghost-types (Gengar, Haunter) are found.
*   **Ruins of Progress:** Long before the Holon researchers arrived, humanity had tried to industrialize the forest. Rusted, abandoned railway tracks and bridges are shown rotting in the jungle undergrowth.
*   **The Borders:** The forest is unnaturally constrained by severe bordering climates—a glacial ice-flow ocean on one side, and highly unstable volcanic calderas on the other.

### 2.2 The Holon Settlement
The village is perfectly circular, resembling a giant Poké Ball from above. 
*   **The Tower & Sky:** The sky above the tower is turbulent and ionized, wracked by severe electrical storms. 
*   **The Facilities:** The interiors are highly sterile and modern. Researchers work behind holographic displays, glass containment units, and banks of glowing blue monitors. 
*   **The Holon Ruins:** Sitting directly outside the high-tech village are the Holon Ruins—primitive stone pillars covered in moss that perfectly match the architectural footprint of the new Tower, proving the site has held power for millennia.

### 2.3 The Holon Lake Wilds
Located south of the city, this sprawling, high-altitude lake looks peaceful on the misty surface but hides a dark secret.
*   **Submerged Industrialization:** Far beneath the water's surface lies a massive network of active, functioning machinery. Mechanical rigs, metallic cubes, and industrial pipes glow in the dark depths alongside mutated aquatic life.
*   **The Corrupted Peaks:** The surrounding jagged mountains are scarred by Holon's expansion. Huge communication antennas are precariously bolted to cliff faces, and isolated columns of EM light regularly pierce the sky from the forest floor.

### 2.4 The Unnamed Island (Crystal Guardians)
An isolated tropical island far from the continent, battered by immense cyclones and windstorms. 
*   **The Glowing Crystals:** The entire island is covered in naturally growing, glowing prismatic crystals, which are said to grant special powers if used correctly.
*   **Biome Adaptation:** The crystals are deeply tied to the earth: Red/Yellow crystals grow in active volcanic fissures; Green/Purple crystals light up massive subterranean cave ceilings; Pale Blue crystals wash up on sandy shores and grow out of ocean coral reefs.

### 2.5 The Dragon Frontiers
The absolute edge of the world, comprised of two primary islands.
*   **The Arid Red Cliffs:** The lower island is extremely harsh, dominated by deep reddish-orange sedimentary canyons and volcanic plateaus. Perfect, pristine standing monoliths sit in the shallow bays here, untouched by Holon scientists.
*   **The Floating Sky Island:** A secondary landmass suspended high above the cloud ceiling. The altitude is so extreme that the atmosphere is completely thin, and the sky appears as a dark, starry outer-space void even in the middle of the day.

## 3. The Delta Species Evolution

The progression of the Delta mutation across the expansions directly correlates to the distance from the Holon Research Tower.

1.  **Holon Settlement:** 50% of families are mutated. Fully evolved δ Delta Species here almost always become dual Metal-type.
2.  **The Fringes (Holon Lake):** Mutation saturation jumps to 96% due to the wild tracking antennas. However, the Metal influence drops to only 39%. Most Pokémon simply undergo a complete base-type change rather than gaining a forced Metal plating.
3.  **Isolated Adaptation (Crystal Island):** The mutation is extremely rare here (26%). Due to the distance, it exhibits a "late-onset" pattern; base-stage Pokémon (like Squirtle) are completely normal, and the Delta mutation only triggers upon reaching their final evolutionary form (Blastoise δ).
4.  **Dragon Frontiers:** Nearly 100% of the Pokémon in this distant region are δ Delta Species. In stark contrast to the Holon Settlement, none of the Pokémon here exhibit a secondary Metal-type.

It is here, in the underwater depths of the Dragon Frontiers, that the researchers finally find the goal of their original expedition: **Mew**—alive, thriving, and naturally mutated into a Water-type Delta Species.

## 4. Notable Figures & Items

*   **The Local Residents:** 
    *   *The Fieldworkers:* The original boots-on-the-ground explorers.
    *   *The Castaway:* A lone stranded sailor surviving via crystal shards on the Unnamed Island.
    *   *The Island Hermit:* An old man living in isolation on the edge of the Dragon Frontiers.
*   **The Specialized Staff:** The Holon settlement operates via distinct ranks: Researchers (lab leads), Scientists (experimentation), Farmers (sustainability), and Mentors (instruction). 
*   **Corporate Overlap:** The presence of characters from the mainline games like **Mr. Stone (Devon Corp)** and **Professor Cozmo**, alongside Professor Elm and Professor Oak, suggests that the Holon Research effort may be heavily bankrolled or monitored by global corporate and scientific conglomerates.

## 1. What Is Holon? — Overview
Holon is a TCG-exclusive location within Mirage Forest. It is a known habitat of and the apparent origin of the unusual δ Delta Species Pokémon. The name Holon comes from "holon," a philosophical concept of something that is simultaneously a whole and a part.

> [!NOTE]
> Though officially referring only to the research village, the term Holon is often used by fans to refer collectively to the locations featured in the TCG sets from EX Delta Species to EX Dragon Frontiers. The English release order does **not** match the Japanese story order. EX Delta Species was released in English before EX Legend Maker, despite EX Legend Maker taking place first in the story.

## 2. Set 1 (Story Order) — EX Legend Maker (Mirage Forest)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese Title** | まぼろしの森 — *Mirage Forest* |

### 2.1 English Blurb
*Every Legend Has a Beginning... What secrets does Mew hide that Pokémon trainers have been waiting to learn? In EX Legend Maker, you'll unravel the truth behind the most mysterious Pokémon of all!*

### 2.2 Setting & Evidence of Prior Civilization
The expansion was set in the Mirage Forest, which is home to many rare Pokémon, including the elusive Mew. The Japanese equivalent set Mirage Forest showed little evidence of human habitation, with the only artificial constructs being a cave shrine, some diamond shaped stone carvings, and a short section of railway. The complementary Mirage's Mew Constructed Starter Deck added a number of cards that feature man-made structures, including a settlement.

### 2.3 Named Landmarks & What the Forest Looks Like
- **Power Spot** — Deep in the forest is a place known as the power spot which is filled with a mysterious power. It was said that when people traveled there, energy welled throughout their body and they were healed. Holon was constructed on top of the power spot in order to study its effects.
- **Cursed Stone** — Depicted as a cave entrance; the Japanese name *Cursed Shrine* indicates that this is the entrance to a shrine.
- **Full Flame** — A small Poké Ball shaped ring of flames in the center of a clearing. The Japanese name *Lasting Flame* suggests that these flames are inextinguishable.
- **Power Tree / Giant Stump** — A giant tree in the center of a clearing, and the cut stump of an even larger tree.
- **Strange Cave** — A cave filled with stalactites and stalagmites, lit by a mysterious soft yellow light.

Mirage Forest is generally depicted as a dense forest with flora reminiscent of the Jurassic period. It is home to Fossil Pokémon and the Mythical Pokémon Mew. The forest's ground is rock-studded in places, and features a number of clearings. It contains an area of dead, leafless trees, and hilly areas of bare rock. It is seemingly located next to the sea, with a beach and pink coral seen on multiple cards. The Sealeo line also features a snowy ice sheet in their cards. Additionally, one card features a smoking volcano within the forest.

### 2.4 Notable Cards, TCG Data & Trivia
The set features Mew and Mew ex as headline cards, as well as Fossil Pokémon including Aerodactyl, Kabuto/Kabutops, and Anorith/Armaldo. The secret rare card is Pikachu δ — the only Delta Species card in the English set. The depiction of Faraway Island in the anime shares many similarities with Mirage Forest and surrounding areas.
*   **Characters/People:** Fieldworkers.
*   **Technology & Items:** Claw Fossil, Mysterious Fossil, Root Fossil, React Energy, Rainbow Energy, Full Flame.

### 2.5 Exhaustive Visual Art & Micro-Biome Analysis
*   **The Deep "Jurassic" Jungle:** **Cradily (3)** and **Lileep** thrive in deep shadows. **Pinsir (24)** clings to massive tree trunks. **Shiftry (12)**, **Nuzleaf**, and **Victreebel** reside in the dark, upper canopies. Pockets of toxic marsh hide **Muk (11)**.
*   **The Power Spot & Mystical Clearings:** Bathed in soft, ethereal pink or yellow dappled light. **Mew (10)** floats peacefully here while Ghost-types like **Gengar (5)** and **Gastly** linger in the shadowy edges.
*   **Volcanic & Arid Outskirts:** **Golem (6)** and **Geodude** sit in barren rocky craters. **Magmar (21)** and **Torkoal (27)** live deep in volcanic calderas amidst active lava streams.
*   **Coral Coast & Abyssal Trenches:** **Gorebyss (17)** swims through shallow reefs with geometric pink and blue coral. **Kabutops (7)** hunts among smooth blue rocks. **Wailord (14)** is found in deep ocean drop-offs.
*   **The Ice Flows:** **Walrein ex (89)** and **Sealeo** rest on floating ice sheets surrounded by freezing water.
*   **The Ruins of Progress:** *Long before Holon arrived*, humanity tried to industrialize the forest. Backgrounds of **Voltorb (68)** and **Electrode** show rusted railway sections actively being reclaimed by the jungle. The **Regice, Regirock, Registeel Stars** reside within crumbling, ancient shrines.

## 3. Set 2 (Story Order) — EX Delta Species (Holon Research Tower)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese Title** | ホロンの研究塔 — *Holon Research Tower* |
| **English Release** | October 31, 2005 |
| **Japanese Release** | October 28, 2005 |

### 3.1 English Blurb
*Behold the Newest Species of Pokémon! From the research labs in the tower of Holon comes a revelation that will thrill anyone who trains and collects Pokémon.*

### 3.2 Setting and Origin of Delta Species
EX Delta Species introduced δ Delta Species and Holon's Pokémon. The expansion was set in the Holon Research Tower, home of scientists who conduct research on Mew and strange new Pokémon. These Pokémon have been altered by a wave of electromagnetic radiation produced by an experiment gone awry at Holon labs. The Japanese source cited by Bulbapedia establishes that the researchers came specifically to search for and study Mew. The Holon Research Tower was built as an electromagnetic tracking tool. The Delta transformation of local Pokémon was an unintended side effect of this research — **not the original goal.**

### 3.3 The Village Layout & The Tower
From above, the village looks like a giant Poké Ball, with a ring of trees serving as the main border and a canal that runs through the diameter into two circles that surround the village center. A river can be seen running past the city. 
At the very center of the village is the **Holon Research Tower**, a triangular tower four stories tall with a domed roof holding a large metal dish facing upwards above it. Three small structures with small metal domes on their roofs surround the tower in a triangle formation. Lightning can be seen arcing between the tower's dish and these domes when active. 

### 3.4 The Mysterious Interior & Ruins
Interior architecture can be seen on multiple cards, sometimes depicted as a normal stone interior, but equally as often depicted as oddly organic looking, containing uneven walls and pillars, as well as a large amount of glowing circular machinery. Holon also contains an abandoned building in the Holon architectural style called the Holon Ruins. No explanation for the ruins is given in the lore.

### 3.5 Delta Species Behavior at Holon
δ Delta Species Pokémon here will almost always become dual Metal-type at their final evolutionary stage and uniquely may never change their base type at all. Those that do change base type will always change to Fire or Lightning type, or rarely to pure Metal type. Of the Holon's Pokémon shown, the Magnemite and Voltorb lines are attracted to and absorb magnetism and electricity. Castform has its cellular structure influenced by changes in climate.

### 3.6 Notable Cards & TCG Data
Introduces Delta Species (Eevee-lutions are all dual Metal). Rayquaza δ, Latias/Latios δ.
*   **Characters/People:** Specialized staff runs the settlement—Holon Researcher, Scientist, Farmer, Lass, Mentor.
*   **Technology & Items:** Holon Transceiver, Dual Ball, Master Ball.
*   **Energies:** Synthetic Holon Energies (FF, GL, WP), Metal Energy, Darkness Energy.

### 3.7 Exhaustive Visual Art & Micro-Biome Analysis
*   **The Tracking Waves:** The pinnacle of the **Holon Research Tower (94)** fires intense golden-orange "tracking waves" into heavily ionized, static-filled skies. **Rayquaza δ (13)** and **Jolteon δ (7)** are seen flying/standing in severe electrical storms caused directly by this EM radiation.
*   **High-Tech Interiors:** Extremely advanced scientific facilities. **Dragonite δ (3)** stands inside an observation unit with a massive glowing holographic ring. **Mewtwo δ (12)** is kept behind reinforced glass in a sterile white containment lab. **Espeon δ (4)** and **Umbreon δ** sit on modern metallic staircases.
*   **The Ruin Paradox:** Just outside the village sit the **Holon Ruins (96)**—primitive, weathered stone pillars overgrown with moss that *perfectly match the architectural footprint of the tower*. 
*   **The Outskirts:** **Tyranitar δ (16)** and **Sandslash δ** live on desolate, rocky jagged plains where the massive Tower looms impossibly large in the far background.

## 6. Set 5 (Story Order) — EX Dragon Frontiers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese Title** | さいはての攻防 — *Offense and Defense of the Furthest Ends* |
| **English Release** | November 8, 2006 |

### 6.1 Setting & Outliers
The expansion was set on an island far away where natural Delta Species Pokémon thrive. The Dragon Frontiers are described as "beyond the horizon." Card art clarifies that there are only two islands: one a rocky island in the ocean, the other a mountainous island floating in the sky.

### 6.2 The Ancient Marks & Holon Legacy
The only artificial structures are diamond carvings similar to those seen in Mirage Forest, a piece of unnaturally square cut stone half submerged in the sea, and a stone circle perfectly identical to Holon Legacy, bridging the architectural styles across the continent. 

### 6.3 Behavior & Lack of Metal Types
Nearly all Pokémon in the Dragon Frontiers are δ Delta Species (100%). Because the Dragon Frontiers are completely immune to the Holon Tower's artificial EM radiation, **ZERO percent of these Delta Pokémon are Metal-type.** They exhibit pure, singular base-type alterations. 

### 6.4 Notable Cards & TCG Data
*   **Characters/People:** Island Hermit, Professor Elm, Professor Oak, Mr. Stone.
*   **Technology & Items:** Buffer Piece, Old Rod, Strength Charm, Copycat, TV Reporter. 
*   **The Goal Realized:** **Mew ★ δ (101)** appears. Mutated naturally into a Water-type Delta Species.

### 6.5 Exhaustive Visual Art & Micro-Biome Analysis
*   **The Arid Red Cliffs:** The lower island is extremely harsh and sun-drenched. **Nidoking δ (6)** and **Trapinch (68)** exist in massive, jagged canyons made of deep reddish-orange sedimentary rock. **Typhlosion δ** roars atop active magma fissures.
*   **The Ancient Monoliths:** The **Island Hermit** stands beside beautifully carved, ancient stone monoliths resting in the shallow water. Other Delta species swim past perfectly square, diamond-carved stones sinking into the sea.
*   **The Floating Sky Island:** **Altaria ex δ (90)** and **Togetic δ (11)** exist here, where the environment is purely jagged rock spires piercing through a sea of fluffy white clouds. 
*   **The Celestial Edge:** The backgrounds of **Rayquaza ex δ (97)** and **Ledian δ (18)** transition into a dark, starry outer-space sky even though it is day, representing the absolute literal boundary of the known world.
*   **The Final Discovery:** **Mew ★ δ (101)** is seen swimming peacefully in a pristine, crystal-clear underwater sea.

## 7. The Holon Stadium Cards — What the Source Actually Says
Each of the TCG sets featuring Delta Species include a Stadium card whose name begins with Holon and which depicts a structure with 3 nodes around a central point, appearing from above like the symbol used on Metal Energy cards. It is unclear what these structures mean, though considering that Holon Research Tower is built on the power spot and is apparently the cause of the local δ Delta Species, it is possible that the symbol represents the location of power spots, with the others existing in the other locations acting as the source of the local Delta Species.

The Bulbapedia Holon page presents two possible interpretations — separate power spots across all locations, or all structures simply referencing back to the Holon Research Tower — and explicitly states neither is confirmed.

## 8. Delta Distribution — Data Overview
Holon, the unnamed island, and the Dragon Frontiers are all outliers in some category. 

| Location | Set | Total δ Families | % Delta | Dual Metal | Base Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Holon | EX Delta Species | 20 / 40 | 50% | 100% of Delta | 55% |
| Holon Lake | EX Holon Phantoms | 23 / 24 | 96% | 39% of Delta | 100% |
| Unnamed Island | EX Crystal Guardians | 10 / 39 | 26% | 40% of Delta | 100% |
| Dragon Frontiers | EX Dragon Frontiers | 42 / 42 | 100% | 0% | 100% |

Pokémon ☆ and non-Legendary/Mythical single stage Pokémon are never δ Delta Species in Holon and the unnamed island, and are always δ Delta Species or Holon's Pokémon in Holon Lake and the Dragon Frontiers. These Pokémon are also never dual Metal-type, though their base type can still be changed to Metal.
