# Gen 1–3 Lore Reference Bank (Verified)

Easter egg source material drawn from Pokémon's own canon, Generations 1–3, plus the Pokémon Adventures manga arcs covering the same period. Lore-level only; placement and implementation undetermined.

Verified against Bulbapedia, September 2026.

---

## Deoxys

- Deoxys's stored state was fixed by the cartridge it lived on rather than by anything the player did. FireRed, LeafGreen, and Emerald each recorded the same organism in a different stable state, with a fourth baseline state in Ruby and Sapphire. Four separate records of the same organism, each internally consistent, none reconcilable with the others.
- Trading a Deoxys between Generation III games recalculated its stored state to match the destination cartridge. The organism settled according to where it was, not what it had been.
- In link battles between two Generation III games, a Deoxys always appeared to the opponent in its baseline state regardless of its actual one. Observation from outside the system returned a default reading.
- Birth Island required an unmarked triangle puzzle solved on bare rock, with no instructions given anywhere in the game.
- The Birth Island Deoxys was flagged internally as a "fateful encounter," a marker invisible in Generation III itself and only surfacing when the Pokémon was transferred to a later generation. A record annotation that becomes legible only after the archive is migrated.
- Birth Island and Navel Rock are both invisible on the Town Map by default, appearing only after the player has physically arrived.
- The Aurora Ticket, Old Sea Map, and Mystic Ticket were distribution-only. The MysticTicket was handed out at Pokémon Festa 2004 and various promotional events across Japan, the United States, and Mexico. Authorized transit that almost nobody legitimately received.
- Deoxys is stated to be a space virus mutated by a laser during atmospheric entry — mutation caused by an external beam is established Pokémon canon, not an invention.
- Deoxys was the first Pokémon to have its own unique battle music.

## Mew and Mewtwo

- The Pokémon Mansion journals read: a new Pokémon discovered deep in the jungle in Guyana, South America; christened Mew on July 10; Mew gave birth on February 6 and the newborn was named Mewtwo; by September 1, Mewtwo is far too powerful and its vicious tendencies have not been curbed. The final entry is an admission of failure filed as a status update.
- In the Japanese text the journals suggest a single researcher working alone. The English localization changes this to a team. The same events read as either one person's obsession or an institution's project depending on which version you have. This is the single most useful entry in the bank for a story about institutional versus individual responsibility.
- The journals exist only in Generation I and its remakes; the Pokémon Mansion was destroyed by the Cinnabar volcano before Generation II. The primary source was lost to a natural event, not a cover-up.
- In Generation VII's version of the mansion, the basement holds glass containment units, the largest of which shows signs of having been struck repeatedly from the inside.
- Mew's Pokédex entries in Pokémon Stadium and FireRed place it in South America. A named real-world location inside an otherwise closed fictional geography.
- Mew on Faraway Island appears as movement in grass and flees on approach, never confirmed by direct sighting.
- Mew is described as containing the genetic composition of all Pokémon.

## Devon and Rustboro

- Devon manufactures Poké Balls, PokéNavs, and Devon Scopes while simultaneously running excavation and research operations.
- Mr. Stone wanted the PokéNav developed specifically so he could better understand Pokémon emotions. A consumer device built to answer a personal question.
- Devon was responsible for carving Rusturf Tunnel through the mountain to connect Route 116 and Verdanturf Town. Infrastructure work halted mid-excavation.
- The Devon Scope reveals something that was standing there the entire time.
- Devon's president negotiates directly with Team Aqua and Magma rather than involving authorities.
- Captain Stern's submarine was stolen and later recovered; the Abandoned Ship off Dewford sat unsalvaged for years.
- Mossdeep Space Center was sited in Mossdeep specifically for its sunny weather and stable winds. The community's wish for safe launches is represented by a white rock. A folk token attached to an engineering facility.
- In Emerald, Team Magma sends the Space Center a letter announcing they will take the rocket fuel, causing a panic, then attacks to seize it after failing to control Groudon. The theft is announced in advance and happens anyway.

## Cozmo and the Meteorite

- Cozmo studied a meteorite at Mt. Chimney; Team Magma took it and used it to amplify volcanic activity.
- Cozmo located the meteorite using a scanner, and explained that it was a fragment of a much larger asteroid. The piece he found was never the whole object.
- He is released unharmed and continues working, which is its own characterization.
- Meteorite fragments in Gen 3 are treated as both scientific specimens and industrial fuel depending on who holds them.

## Jirachi

- Jirachi wakes for seven days once every thousand years when the Millennium Comet passes.
- It grants wishes written on the tags attached to its head.
- Its true eyes remain closed even while active.
- Jirachi grants exactly three wishes before returning to sleep. A hard limit on a functionally unlimited power.

## The Regis and Braille

- The Regi puzzles are the only ones in Ruby, Sapphire, and Emerald that the games refuse to translate. The instructions are carved in braille, printed as bare patterns of dots, and no NPC ever reads them aloud.
- The decoder chart shipped on a paper insert with the physical game. The outer room of the Sealed Chamber reproduces that same alphabet chart on its floor — the developers put the manual inside the ruin.
- Reading the inner inscription with Relicanth at the top of the party and Wailord at the bottom triggers an earthquake that opens three doors in three separate locations the player cannot see. Emerald reverses the order.
- The chamber's text says the Pokémon were sealed because "We feared it." The English translation's singular pronoun has led some to read this as referring to a fourth entity rather than the trio. The Japanese specifies no subject at all, singular or plural. A translation artifact that generated a decade of interpretation.
- The giants were sealed away across the world by humanity. Not by their creator, and not by accident.
- The Sealed Chamber is modeled on the Yonaguni Monument, a real submerged rock formation that some argue is artificial and others argue is natural. The source material is itself a live archaeological dispute.
- Relicanth is based on the coelacanth, closely related to the ancestors of land vertebrates; Wailord is based on the whale, whose ancestors left the land for the sea. The key to the ruin is a creature that came ashore and a creature that went back.

## Ancient Ruins

- A Ruin Maniac states the Tanoby Ruins have stood for over 1,500 years, for reasons unknown.
- The seven Tanoby Chambers are named Monean, Liptoo, Weepth, Dilford, Scufib, Rixy, and Viapois, each with a differently colored top band. In the Japanese version they are named for the haru no nanakusa, the seven flowers of spring traditionally eaten at the start of the year. Ancient chambers named after a seasonal meal.
- The Unown are a written alphabet that behaves as an organism.
- The Ruins of Alph emit a "Mysterious Transmission" audible on radio channel 13.5 while near or inside them. Scientists frequent the area listening to the ruins, and suggest the puzzles in the caves are connected to whatever the transmission is. A structure that broadcasts, studied by people who cannot decode the signal.
- Braille appears as an untranslated script in unconnected regions — Hoenn's Sealed Chamber and Regi chambers, and the Sevii Islands' Dotted Hole and Mt. Ember cave.
- The Dotted Hole takes its name from the six braille dots on its door, and the pillars and door use real braille symbols.
- Mt. Pyre's summit holds the Red and Blue Orbs, guarded by people who inherited the duty rather than chose it.

## Weather Trio

- The Sootopolis legend holds that Groudon and Kyogre fought until Rayquaza descended and ended it, then returned to the sky.
- Rayquaza lives in the ozone layer and has been there for hundreds of millions of years, feeding on water and particles in the atmosphere.
- In the manga, stopping the crisis requires riding Rayquaza to the upper atmosphere, and Steven recalls that Rayquaza emits ozone — which prompts Mr. Stone to remember a set of protective suits already in development at Devon. The company happened to have the equipment for an event nobody planned for.
- The Sky Pillar's floors collapse under weight; the ascent is structurally hostile by design.
- Groudon and Kyogre were awakened by people who read ancient warnings as instructions.

## Legendary Birds and Beasts

- Lugia is described as living at the bottom of the sea because its wingbeats can level houses — a creature hiding for others' safety.
- Ho-Oh burned in the Brass Tower and revived three Pokémon that died in the fire, creating the beasts.
- Navel Rock's topographical location is described as a closely guarded secret, on the reasoning that if the information reached the wrong person it would be a disaster. The Mystic Ticket is presented as something given to those deemed worthy. Access controlled by judgment rather than by lock.
- Lugia roosts in the depths of Navel Rock and Ho-Oh at its summit — the same rock reaching far below the water and far above it.
- The Kanto birds were dormant until disturbed; none of them sought contact.

## Lati@s and Southern Island

- The stone at the center of Southern Island's forest carries a single inscription: *All dreams are but another reality. Never forget...* No context, no author, no explanation.
- Latios and Latias can render themselves invisible by refracting light.
- The Eon Pokémon on Southern Island is always holding a Soul Dew — the only legitimate way to obtain the item in Generation III.
- In Ruby and Sapphire, once the island is left it cannot be returned to, even with the ticket still in hand. A place that admits you once.
- Which Eon Pokémon roams Hoenn is decided by the player's answer to their mother's question about a color seen on television. A legendary's location determined by a piece of household small talk.

## Orre

- Ein, Cipher's head scientist, describes the Shadow process as "shutting the door to their hearts." The process itself is never disclosed.
- In XD, Cipher's science division refines and upgrades the process so that many Shadow Pokémon can be produced simultaneously. The step from single specimen to production line.
- Colosseum's Shadow Pokémon were deemed failures internally because they could be purified by simple means. The explicit goal in XD was an unpurifiable one.
- Shadow Lugia, codenamed XD001, was created by the Cipher Admin Lovrina and believed to be completely immune to purification. Its appearance differs from a normal Lugia — jagged silver eye-spikes, dark-purple body, glowing red eyes, clawed toes — unlike every other Shadow Pokémon, which look normal and merely carry a dark aura. Corruption that became visible in the body.
- Shadow Lugia was supposed to deliver the S.S. Libra to Cipher's headquarters. An instability in the corruption process let it briefly break free of control, and it dropped the ship in the middle of the desert instead.
- The claim of immunity was false. Shadow Lugia could be purified by maxing out the tempo of all nine sets of the Purification Chamber. The scientist who built it was wrong about what she had built.
- Shadow Lugia cannot enter Reverse Mode.
- Orre had almost no wild Pokémon at all — an ecologically dead region.

## Team Rocket, Aqua, and Magma

- Team Rocket ran Silph Co. as an occupation, not a raid.
- Silph Co. developed the Master Ball and the Silph Scope in the same building.
- Team Rocket's scientist Gideon steals the Sapphire from the Dotted Hole and physically blocks access to the ruin until driven off.
- The Pokémon Mansion was abandoned mid-experiment and never cleaned up.
- Aqua and Magma each recruited normal people who believed the stated goal was reasonable.
- Both organizations had internal documents; neither had a plan for success.

## Fossils and Revival

- Cinnabar's lab revives fossils on request, commercially, with no ethical framework mentioned anywhere.
- The Root and Claw Fossils are found in a desert that was once underwater.
- Aerodactyl was revived from amber containing preserved genetic material.

## The Battle Frontier

- The Hoenn Battle Frontier is owned and operated by Scott privately. He lives in a house between the Battle Dome and Battle Tower. A major institution that is one man's property.
- The seven Frontier Brains are Trainers Scott hired. Not appointed by any league, not elected, not inherited.
- Entering the Frontier upgrades the player's Trainer Card into a Frontier Pass, which stores their record and symbols. Only one recorded battle can be saved at a time; recording another overwrites it. An archive with room for exactly one memory.
- Frontier Brains hold titles — Factory Head, Pyramid King, Salon Maiden — rather than positional ranks.

## Bill and the Storage System

- Bill invented the original Pokémon Storage System and the Time Capsule. In Red, Blue, and Yellow he is first found having accidentally combined himself with the body of a Pokémon during a teleportation experiment, and needs the player's help to reverse it. The man who built the system for digitizing living things tested it on himself and got it wrong.
- Until the player meets him, the PC menu reads "Someone's PC." Every trainer's stored Pokémon are held by a person whose name they may never learn.
- Every regional storage system — Lanette's, Bebe's, Amanita's, Cassius's — derives directly or indirectly from Bill's original.
- FireRed and LeafGreen establish that PCs require special systems to connect between distant locations, which is why the Ruby and Sapphire gems matter at all.

## Mirage Island

- Every day the game generates a random number between 0 and 65535. If it matches the personality value of any Pokémon in the player's party, Mirage Island appears on Route 130 that day. The number is never disclosed to the player.
- An old man in Pacifidlog Town tells the player each day whether the island is currently visible. A daily report on a phenomenon he cannot influence or explain.
- The island holds wild Wynaut and the only Liechi Berry tree in the game.
- According to Juan, Mirage Island is not the island's actual name — it is simply what people call it.

## Miscellaneous Deep Cuts

- The Berry glitch in early Ruby and Sapphire cartridges froze the internal calendar 366 days after first play. Berries stopped growing, but so did Mirage Island's daily number, Route 119's weather, the department store sales, the lottery drawings, and the NPCs who hand out berries. The Mossdeep Space Center's count of successful rocket launches reset to 1. A single clock failure that quietly stopped an entire world's daily life, noticed first because a plant would not grow.
- Nintendo acknowledged it, published mitigation methods, and folded a permanent fix into later production runs. The in-game distribution NPC delivered a corporate patch by standing in a room and talking to you.
- Porygon is explicitly a program that can move through electronic space.
- The Cinnabar Gym's quiz doors required correct answers about Pokémon biology before granting passage.
- Lavender Town's tower was a graveyard before it became a radio station.
- The Pokémon Tower Marowak is a ghost that must be identified before it can be fought.
- The Safari Zone operates on a timer and a step count, and Pokémon flee for reasons never explained.
- The Seafoam Islands and Cerulean Cave were both left open with no explanation of what maintains them.
- Cerulean Cave's Mewtwo is guarded by a man who only steps aside once you have proven yourself to an institution.
- The Trainer Tower in the Sevii Islands existed purely as a private endurance facility.
- Berry Master NPCs speak in phrases that generate specific berry types — language as a growth input.
- The Bicycle Shop gives a bicycle away for free on a voucher and never explains why.
- The Move Deleter and Move Reminder are individuals with the power to edit memory permanently.
- The Day Care returns Pokémon knowing moves they were never taught, and the attendants claim not to know how.
- The Name Rater's entire profession is judging whether a name should stand.
- Pokémon that hatch from eggs are stated to be at level 5 with no explanation of the missing developmental window.
- A Pokémon's personality value is assigned in halves at different moments — the lower half when the Day-Care Man finds the Egg, the upper half when the player collects it. Identity written in two sittings by two different parties.
- The Union Room and Trade Corner made Pokémon transferable property with no records kept.
- Regional Pokédex numbering and National numbering disagree, meaning two institutions catalogued the same animals independently.

# Pokémon Adventures (Manga) — Gen 1–3 Arcs

The manga is a parallel continuity, not canon to the games, which makes it ideal Easter egg material: recognizable to readers, deniable to everyone else. The Deoxys material in particular sits almost on top of the existing premise.

## The Two Deoxys

- There are two Deoxys in Adventures, called Organism No. 1 and Organism No. 2. They are twins, created when viruses floating in space were exposed to strange radiation. After mutating, they attached themselves to a meteor, which crashed into Hoenn and was found by Professor Cozmo, who took it to the Mossdeep Space Center for study. During Courtney's attack on the Space Center, Team Rocket stole both organisms in the confusion. Radiation mutation, a meteor impact, Cozmo recovering it, an institution studying it, and a covert party taking it — the whole chain already exists in published Pokémon fiction.
- Team Rocket sacrificed one of the two to make the other stronger.
- Organism No. 2 broke free of captivity and, while rampaging through Team Rocket's vault, absorbed a sample of Red's blood. This formed a connection between them and led the creature to believe Red was its ancestor. A stored biological sample producing an unintended relationship years later.
- Giovanni had collected various cell samples specifically to strengthen his forces. One was blood Red left behind after their battle five years earlier. The archive was assembled deliberately; what it did was not.
- Organism No. 2, still incomplete, retreated to Birth Island after escaping in order to finish its growth and wake itself. Giovanni's response on learning this was that Birth Island was a perfect place for it to do so.
- Deoxys's stability in Adventures depends on the Ruby and Sapphire stones. Bill and Celio retrieving them on Five Island is what leaves the creature locked and weakened. The same gems that connect the storage networks also govern the organism's energy state.
- Organism No. 2 could produce clones of itself, an ability Giovanni claims it gained after its capture.
- Organism No. 1 was invisible, detectable only by Green's Silph Scope after an upgrade extended it to all invisible Pokémon. An existing instrument modified slightly turns out to be the only thing that can see the problem.
- This Deoxys sets off to find a region with meteorites that would let it stabilize itself on its own terms.

## Cipher, Sird, and the Unown

- Sird unlocked the secret of the Tanoby Key and gained control of the Unown in the Tanoby Ruins, using them to defeat Lorelei, spy on Deoxys's movements, and guard the Trainer Tower against intruders. A 1,500-year-old ruin repurposed as a surveillance and security system.
- Sird had infiltrated Team Rocket in order to capture Deoxys for Cyrus. An operative inside one organization the whole time, working for a different one.

## The Sealed Chamber and Devon

- In Adventures the Sealed Chamber's inscription is not on a wall but on a damaged stone tablet, entrusted by Steven to a blind boy. Phoebe retrieves it after the boy cannot work out the damaged section. The one person suited to read a tactile script is defeated by the part that is missing.
- The letter Mr. Stone asked Sapphire to deliver to Steven turns out to contain a reconstruction of the tablet's damaged paragraph. The corporation held the missing piece of the ancient record and moved it by courier.
- Sapphire delivers the letter mid-crisis, which is what allows Steven to awaken the Legendary Golems.

## Mirage Island

- Time on Mirage Island advances at a different pace than the outside world. Ruby and Sapphire wash up there and lose 21 days while unconscious. The island is not merely hard to find; it runs on a different clock.

## Bill

- In the manga Bill fuses specifically with a Rattata. The accident happens because his shirt catches the door and pulls the machine shut with him inside; it activates automatically and swaps him with the Rattata in the receiving unit. A catastrophic identity failure caused by clothing.

## The Battle Frontier and Jirachi

- Emerald is sent by Professor Oak and Crystal to catch Jirachi within seven days — the window matching Jirachi's own waking period.
- Noland captured three Smeargle from Artisan Cave after noticing they had witnessed Jirachi and copied its Wish. Using the light from the Smeargle's Wish, he found a way to predict where Jirachi would appear next. An organism that copies moves becomes an instrument for tracking a legendary.
- Guile Hideout captured Jirachi and used a wish to summon a giant Kyogre-shaped mass of water to sink the Battle Frontier. A wish-granting creature used as a weapons platform.
- Scott spent the last of Jirachi's three wishes summoning thousands of visitors to the Battle Frontier. The final wish of a millennial creature, spent on foot traffic.
- Latias and Latios accompany Emerald throughout as his close friends — directly relevant to a Heroes of Alto Mare postgame.

## Mewtwo

- After the airship battle, Mewtwo notes that where it came from caused it distress, but since it was created from Mew's eyelash it has a place it can trace its roots back to. Manufactured origin treated as ancestry rather than as a defect.
- Mew stops the Team Rocket airship from crashing, and Mewtwo wonders whether it came because it could sense that Mewtwo was there.

## Mr. Stone

- Mr. Stone apologizes for having used the power of science to capture Rayquaza — acknowledging that Devon believed the Draconid legends were true but never considered whether they had the Draconids' consent to take the sacred dragon.
- Zinnia refuses the apology, telling him he is arrogant and that nothing will change his mind about saving the planet at the cost of many people. A corporate apology delivered sincerely and rejected on the grounds that sincerity was never the issue.
