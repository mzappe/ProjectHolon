## Part I — The Foundation

### I.1 What Holon Is

Holon takes its name from the philosophical concept of a holon — something that is simultaneously a whole and a part of something larger. This is not flavor. It is the precise dynamical reality of how the game's disaster unfolds.

Every system in Holon is complete and self-sufficient at its own scale. The Tower's research program is legitimate science. The ancient civilization's management of the crystal network maintained regional stability for thousands of years. Rayquaza's territorial aggression is ecologically correct. Each of these systems, examined within its own frame, is working exactly as intended. This is the first problem: a system operating correctly at its own scale has no internal signal that it is embedded in something larger. Success removes the pressure to look outward. The blind spot is not a failure of intelligence — it is a structural consequence of competence within a bounded frame.

But scale-blindness alone does not produce the disaster. The second principle is sensitivity. Complex systems — networks of interacting agents, feedback loops, coupled dynamics — do not respond to perturbations proportionally. A small disturbance introduced at the right point, in the right conditions, does not stay small. It propagates. It amplifies. It crosses scales. None of the outcomes in this story were latent in their causes. They emerged from the interactions between agents operating at different scales simultaneously — each one doing exactly what it does, none of them intending what results.

The flip side of sensitivity is emergence. The same complexity that makes a system catastrophically sensitive to perturbation also allows it to produce behaviors and capacities that none of its components contain alone. The disaster and its detection are products of the same interconnection. The connections that generate the crisis run in both directions.

This is why the player is the only one who can assemble the full picture. Every other character operates at their own resolution, correctly, within their own frame. The player is the only agent that traverses all scales — and traversal is the only way to see what the systems are doing to each other. Where the player does this with company — Cozmo, Steven, and others — rather than alone, that collaborative synthesis is its own distinct mode of discovery, woven through the world and story documents as the player's science team putting evidence together in real time.

### I.2 Deoxys

A mutation of an extraterrestrial virus carried to Earth on an ancient comet, buried deep beneath what became the Holon region. Not a Pokémon in the conventional sense — no evolutionary relatives anywhere on Earth. Can rewrite its own DNA in real time, cycling between four formes. While dormant, takes the form of a stone tablet.

Deoxys's passive EM biology, expressed over millennia from beneath the ground, produced two things: the **Power Spot** — mysterious, healing energy radiating upward through the earth above the crash site — and the **crystal network**. Deoxys's energy doesn't move through rock indiscriminately; like charged particles channeled by a planetary magnetic field, it propagates along the field's actual directional structure, concentrating wherever the local field geometry funnels it most strongly. The crystal network grew over millennia at exactly those points of concentration — not because crystals are magnetic, but because that's where the energy was strong and sustained enough, for long enough, to alter the geology.

**Deoxys as planetary stabilizer:** A dormant, intact Deoxys naturally regulates the regional and planetary magnetic field as a consequence of its own biology — not a choice, simply what it does. The ancient civilization observed across generations that the region was stable and attributed this to a guardian beneath the land. What nobody understood, including the ancient civilization, is that this stabilizing influence extended to planetary scale: Deoxys's intact presence was a form of protection against solar EM events. Its destabilization by the Tower incident removed that protection. The magnetic shield has been weakening ever since.

**Delta Deoxys:** When the Tower's escalated broadcast drove a pulse of EM radiation downward into the ground, Deoxys woke inside a magnetic environment completely unlike the one it went dormant in. The Tower's years of broadcasting had corrupted the regional magnetic baseline. Deoxys woke into that corruption and its forme-cycling mechanism expressed through a contaminated field it had no framework for — producing the **Delta Deoxys** state. It escaped through the Tower facility, and its whereabouts in the region are unknown to everyone.

### I.3 The Ancient Civilization

The people who lived in Holon before the researchers arrived. They are gone — no explanation exists in the TCG, and that silence is preserved here. What they left behind is the evidence of how they understood the place they lived.

**What they knew:** The Delta phenomenon predates the Tower by millennia. The ancient civilization found it, recognized its source in the crystal network, and built an entire culture around tending to it carefully. They didn't know about Deoxys specifically — they knew about the guardian. They understood what was buried beneath the power spot was significant and chose not to disturb it.

Their understanding operated on two layers. The first — ecological — is legible in everything they built: the crystal network managed the spread of the Delta phenomenon, contained it within the region's historical borders, and maintained species stability across the landscape.

The second layer is deeper. The civilization understood that the crystal network, by virtue of its sensitivity to EM radiation across its full extent, functioned as a **planetary-scale observatory** — capable of reading the health of the magnetic field and detecting signals from beyond the region. They built their sites along the network's strongest nodes specifically to read these signals. Dragon Frontiers — where the sky is thinnest and the planetary shielding weakest — was their most powerful observation point, and the site where their deepest records are kept. They knew the guardian's influence extended beyond the region. They knew what a compromised guardian would mean for the world above it.

**What they built:** Their sites span the entire region — Mirage Forest through Dragon Frontiers — positioned at the points where the regional field's geometry concentrates Deoxys's energy most strongly, the same points where the crystal network grew densest. Every structure reflects the same principle: build around power, not through it.

| Site | Location | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Diamond carvings | Mirage Forest + Dragon Frontiers | A paired record system spanning the whole region. Mirage Forest carvings document the guardian's arrival and the Delta phenomenon's origin. Dragon Frontiers carvings document the cosmic layer — the guardian's role in planetary protection, and what its absence would mean. Together they form the complete picture the player assembles. |
| Stone Dome Grove | Holon Lake | Large ancient domes with geometric carvings; trees growing through circular openings in the stone. Positioned at a strong crystal network node. Still structurally intact. |
| The Holon Legacy | Dragon Frontiers | A stone circle — three tall standing stones around a shorter central stone. The Japanese TCG name translates as *Holon's Holy Site*. The furthest point from Holon that still carries its name. Built at the civilization's most sensitive observation point. |
| Holon Ruins | Holon Village | An abandoned building whose stone pillars perfectly match the architectural footprint of the Tower — evidence this site has held significance for millennia, predating the expedition entirely. |
| The Sunken Stone | Dragon Frontiers coast | A massive worked stone block, cut at right angles, half-submerged in the ocean. Artificial, enormous, anomalous. Purpose unclear. |
| Regi Chambers | Mirage Forest | Three sealed chambers housing Regirock, Regice, and Registeel. Deliberately sealed — the same people who built around the power spot understood some things should be left undisturbed. |
| The Holon Circle | Crystal Isles | A shaped ring of crystals at the network's densest node — the most visible surviving example of how the ancient civilization worked with the crystal network. A boundary structure designed to lock energy within a specific area. |

**What they understood that the expedition doesn't:** The crystal network is a living system, not passive geology. It is both an ecological containment structure and a planetary EM observatory. The phenomenon contained itself for thousands of years because someone was tending it. The researchers inherited that system without knowing it existed.

**What is not yet developed about this civilization** — the actual content of their belief system (what they believed the "guardian" was, and how organized or religious that belief was), what their architecture and visual language communicates about their values beyond "build around power, not through it," whether they had a name for themselves or for Deoxys, what caused their disappearance, and how much of this the player can piece together versus what remains permanently illegible even after full exploration. See Open Questions.

### I.4 The Crystal Network

**What TCG canon establishes:** A mysterious island in the Crystal Isles bears naturally growing crystals said to grant special powers if used correctly. The Holon Circle — a shaped ring of crystals — is a named canonical artifact. The Cessation Crystal and Mysterious Shard are named items with distinct measurable properties.

**What the game establishes:** The crystals are not native to Crystal Isles alone — they appear across the entire region as a pre-existing network, seeded over millennia at the points where the regional magnetic field's geometry channeled Deoxys's dormant EM biology most strongly — the same way a planetary field channels charged particles toward the poles. The ancient civilization didn't build the network. They found it, recognized what it was, and built their culture around maintaining the conditions that sustained it.

The network has two functions operating simultaneously:

**Ecological function:** The crystal network conducts and contains the Delta phenomenon — managing its spread, moderating its intensity, maintaining the historical boundaries beyond which the transformation didn't naturally propagate. Without active tending, the network allows the phenomenon to spread outward.

**Observatory function:** The crystal network is exquisitely sensitive to EM radiation at every scale — local, regional, and planetary. Across its full extent, it functions as a passive detector of magnetic field health and incoming EM events. The ancient civilization read the network the way a modern scientist reads an instrument array. Crystal Isles, with the densest crystal concentration due to its receptive geology, was the most sensitive node. Dragon Frontiers, where the planetary shielding is thinnest, was where the signal from beyond the atmosphere came through most clearly.

Devon's underwater infrastructure — the submerged machinery at Holon Lake, the pipework extending outward from the Tower — was built where the regional magnetic field is strongest, a straightforward engineering decision about where to place equipment for maximum signal. Without realizing it, Devon built directly into the same points where the field channels Deoxys's energy and where the crystal network grew densest as a result. Epsilon's expansion of that infrastructure has dramatically increased the network's sensitivity and reach — accidentally constructing the most powerful EM observatory on the planet, without knowing either that the observatory function existed or what it is now detecting.

### I.5 The Aurora Phenomenon

The region experiences a marked surge in aurora borealis activity, vivid and frequent enough that it becomes a topic of common conversation among researchers and NPCs well before anyone understands its cause. The aurora is a visible, naked-eye symptom of the same underlying EM activity the crystal network's observatory function tracks instrumentally — it is the planetary signal made directly observable, without any need for instruments to detect it.

In the absence of better information, the natural and widely held assumption is that the surge is connected to Deoxys — its disturbance, its EM biology, its movement through the region. This is a reasonable theory given everything else known about Deoxys, and it holds for some time before being corrected.

The correction comes later, once a specific aurora color or pattern is identified as attributable to Deoxys's Delta transformation specifically — distinguishable from the broader surge. Once Deoxys's actual contribution is understood as a narrow signature within the aurora rather than its dominant cause, it becomes clear the bulk of the surge was never primarily about Deoxys. The real driver is the same rising solar activity independently tracked at the Volcano Observatory. The aurora and the observatory data are two independent lines of evidence converging on the same answer, arriving through completely different registers — one visible to anyone looking at the sky, the other requiring instruments and expertise.

The exact mechanism of the correction — whether the player witnesses the distinct Deoxys-signature aurora directly, whether an NPC identifies and explains it, or whether the player pieces it together by comparing sightings — is not yet resolved. See Open Questions.

## Part II — The World

### II.1 The Region at a Glance

The Holon Research Tower has been broadcasting for years. Its magnetic field slowly transformed local Pokémon into Delta Species — a side effect the research team discovered without understanding they caused it. The Tower was built to find Mew. What the Tower's operation revealed, over time, is that the broadcast was amplifying a magnetic field already present in the landscape. The power spot at the region's center is not geology. Something is buried there — and it has been there far longer than anyone in the expedition imagined.

The Tower incident occurred in the months before the player arrives: an escalated broadcast drove a pulse downward into the ground, waking what was buried. The explosion destroyed part of the Tower and was covered up as an equipment malfunction. The Tower has been repaired and is back online. The thing that escaped is loose in the region. The magnetic shield that protected the planet has been weakening since.

**The opening:** The player arrives by boat to a coastal entrance town [TBD: name] as a Devon expedition participant, recruited by personal letter from Steven Stone. Everything on arrival looks like a legitimate research posting. Most people here believe that's what it is.

Professor Cozmo receives the player, and Ty — who arrived on the same boat — and sets the expedition's framing: the power spot, the Tower's history, the Delta phenomenon, the Mew search. He gives the player their starter — one of three rare Delta specimens from the farthest reaches of the region: **Delta Dratini (Grass)**, **Delta Ralts (Fire)**, or **Delta Bagon (Water)**. [TBD: what Ty receives.]

The player's stated goals: document Delta Species across all five zones, fill the Pokédex, pursue the Mew search, complete the Holon Pokémon League.

### II.2 The Five Zones

#### Mirage Forest
*TCG source: EX Legend Maker (Japanese: まぼろしの森)*

A dense forest with flora reminiscent of the Jurassic period. The player's starting zone. Home to Fossil Pokémon roaming freely — the direct result of Devon's fossil restoration program — and the power spot buried at its center, above which Holon Village was built. The forest's biomes shift unexpectedly — dense canopy gives way to a Sand Basin, temperature drops near an Ice Plateau, clearings open without warning. The forest is bordered by a glacial ocean on one side and smoking volcanic calderas on the other.

**Named TCG landmarks:** The Power Spot (a clearing brimming with mysterious healing energy); the Cursed Shrine (cave entrance); the Full Flame / Lasting Flame (an inextinguishable ring of flames in a clearing); the Power Tree (a giant tree alongside the stump of an even larger one); the Strange Cave (stalactites and stalagmites lit by soft yellow light). A short section of rusted railway — humanity's earlier failed attempt to industrialize the forest, now reclaimed by jungle.

**Card-level visual reference:**
- *The deep jungle:* Cradily and Lileep thrive in deep shadow. Pinsir clings to massive tree trunks. Shiftry, Nuzleaf, and Victreebel in dark upper canopies. Muk inhabits pockets of toxic marsh.
- *The Power Spot and mystical clearings:* Bathed in soft ethereal pink or yellow dappled light. Mew floats peacefully here. Ghost-types — Gengar, Gastly — linger at the shadowy edges.
- *Volcanic and arid outskirts:* Golem and Geodude occupy barren rocky craters. Magmar and Torkoal live in volcanic calderas amid active lava streams.
- *Coral coast and abyssal trenches:* Gorebyss swims through shallow reefs with geometric pink and blue coral. Kabutops hunts among smooth blue rocks. Wailord in deep ocean drop-offs.
- *The ice flows:* Walrein ex and Sealeo rest on floating ice sheets in freezing water.
- *Ruins of progress:* Rusted railway sections actively reclaimed by jungle. The Regi Chambers reside within crumbling ancient shrines.

**Key TCG lore:** The researchers came here specifically searching for Mew. The forest's Jurassic character and the presence of Fossil Pokémon alongside Mew are established in the set. Diamond carvings — the first half of the ancient civilization's paired record system — are found here. The English blurb: *Every Legend Has a Beginning. What secrets does Mew hide that Pokémon trainers have been waiting to learn?*

**Delta behavior:** Sparse. The forest's Delta presence predates the Tower, carried by the crystal network rather than the Tower's broadcast. The first encounter with the phenomenon before its full expression is understood.

#### Holon Village & the Research Tower
*TCG source: EX Delta Species (Japanese: ホロンの研究塔)*

The research village built around and above the power spot. Viewed from above, the layout resembles a giant Poké Ball — a ring of trees as the outer border, a canal running through the diameter into two circular waterways around the village center. Nine buildings with sloped red roofs, many with farms or orchards.

The Holon Research Tower stands at the absolute center: a four-storey triangular tower with a large upward-facing metal dish above its domed roof. Three smaller domed satellite structures surround it in a triangle formation; lightning arcs between them when active. Viewed from above, the Tower and satellites form the Metal Energy symbol. The Tower has been broadcasting continuously since construction — firing intense golden-orange tracking waves into heavily ionized, static-filled skies. Signs of recent repair are visible to anyone paying attention.

The Holon Ruins stand elsewhere in the village: an abandoned building whose stone pillars are overgrown with moss and perfectly match the architectural footprint of the Tower. The site has held significance for millennia. Devon's researchers know about it and cannot date it.

**Key TCG lore:** The electromagnetic field produced by the Tower's experiment is the established cause of Delta transformation in the immediate area. Holon's Pokémon — Magnemite, Voltorb, Castform lines — are drawn or affected by the magnetic field due to their existing biological properties. Specialized staff runs the settlement: Holon Researcher, Scientist, Farmer, Mentor.

**Card-level visual reference:**
- *The Tower in operation:* Fires intense golden-orange tracking waves into heavily ionized, static-filled skies. Rayquaza δ and Jolteon δ are visible in the severe electrical storms.
- *High-tech interiors:* Advanced scientific facilities. Dragonite δ stands inside an observation unit with a massive glowing holographic ring. Mewtwo δ is held behind reinforced glass in a sterile white containment lab.
- *The ruin paradox:* Holon Ruins — primitive weathered stone pillars overgrown with moss perfectly matching the Tower's footprint.
- *The outskirts:* Tyranitar δ and Sandslash δ on desolate rocky plains with the Tower looming in the far background.

**Delta behavior:** Almost all fully evolved Pokémon gain a Metal secondary type. The Metal secondary is the Tower's specific fingerprint — it does not appear in areas the Tower's signal doesn't reach directly.

#### Holon Lake
*TCG source: EX Holon Phantoms (Japanese: ホロンの幻影)*

South of Holon Village. "The unknown lands beyond the labs of Holon." A large lake surrounded by deciduous forest with an intersecting mountain range of jagged blue rock; the far end fades into scattered stone spikes. Closer to the lake: marshland and long grasses. Far beneath the water's surface lies a massive network of active, functioning industrial machinery — mechanical rigs, metallic cubes, and industrial pipes. Huge communication antennas are bolted precariously to the highest cliff faces. Isolated columns of EM light regularly pierce the sky from the forest floor.

Two significant ancient structures: the **Stone Dome Grove** — large domes covered in geometric carvings, each containing a tree with branches growing through circular openings in the stone, still structurally intact — and the **Lake Structure**, a formation of three stone pillars in a triangle around a central stone sphere that glows bright blue when active. Both structures are positioned at crystal network nodes.

**Key TCG lore:** The Delta behavior here is anomalous — Meowth δ is dual Metal-type before fully evolving; Magikarp δ starts as Metal and evolves into Lightning/Metal rather than the established progression. The Holon Adventurer card shows an explorer discovering the stone domes, implying they are not common knowledge. Characters appearing here include Mr. Stone and Professor Cozmo. Named items: Mr. Stone's Project, Professor Cozmo's Discovery. Also notable: this is the location where Rayquaza δ appears in TCG card art — a single-type Fire Delta with no Metal secondary, depicted swimming past the submerged industrial network.

**Card-level visual reference:**
- *The lake surface:* Vast and misty. Smooth ancient Stone Domes partially submerged in the shallows.
- *The submerged industrial network:* Gyarados δ and Rayquaza δ swim past massive networks of active submerged industrial pipes, metallic cubes, and glowing mechanical drilling rigs.
- *The encroaching tech of the wilds:* Aerodactyl δ and Pidgeot δ fly past huge communication antennas bolted to the highest jagged cliffs.
- *Pillars of light:* Mysterious vertical beams of light shooting up from the forest floor into the sky — the Tower's frequency bleeding outward through the crystal network.

**Delta behavior:** Nearly all Pokémon are Delta Species (96%). Fewer dual-types than at Holon Village because the Tower's influence is weaker at this distance — but the anomalous pre-evolution Metal typing indicates a different underlying cause operating alongside the Tower's broadcast.

#### Crystal Isles
*TCG source: EX Crystal Guardians (Japanese: きせきの結晶 — Miracle Crystal)*

A remote tropical volcanic archipelago reached by boat. Rocky coastlines and palm-lined beaches. Interior is jungle-like with Jurassic-similar flora mixed with tall thin conifers. Multiple smoking volcanoes. The island suffers from severe windstorms — massive swirling grey cyclones regularly batter the upper altitudes. The most striking feature: glowing crystals cover the entire island, adapting in color and form to their biome — red and orange in volcanic fissures, green and purple in subterranean cave ceilings, pale blue growing from sandy shores and coral reefs.

The **Holon Circle** — a circular crater containing three tall pink-purple crystals in a triangle formation — is the named canonical landmark and the most powerful surviving example of how the ancient civilization managed the crystal network. The Cessation Crystal and Mysterious Shard are named items with distinct measurable properties.

The **Volcano Observatory** sits on the rim of the largest active volcano, built on volcanic rock with an unusually low magnetic signature. The crystals here act as natural EM amplifiers — this is the most sensitive observation node in the accessible region, and the point where the crystal network's observatory function is most legible. As Epsilon's infrastructure expansion increases the network's reach and density, the observatory's sensitivity increases with it. This is the home of the Castaway across the main game — see IV.5.

**Key TCG lore:** A mysterious island far from Holon where both regular and Delta Pokémon coexist. The last set to feature dual-type Delta Pokémon. Characters appearing here include The Castaway (a researcher surviving via crystal shards). English blurb: *Miracle Crystal.*

**Card-level visual reference:**
- *Volcanic highlands:* Active volcanic regions with jagged rock and intense red/orange glowing crystals. Camerupt and Charizard δ inhabit these zones.
- *Cyclone skies:* Massive swirling grey cyclones, extreme turbulence, and rapid lightning discharges constantly battering the upper altitudes.
- *Crystal beach and deep marine:* Pale blue ice-like crystals grow from wet sand and swamps. Deep in the ocean, Luvdisc swims past glowing blue crystals growing out of coral reefs.
- *Subterranean darkness:* Deep underground caves where massive clusters of purple and green crystals provide the only light source.

**Delta behavior:** Least prevalent here among the five zones (26% of families). Changes appear only at final evolution and still carry the Metal secondary — the last location where the Tower's fingerprint holds. The crystals interact with the phenomenon in ways distinct from the Tower's direct broadcast.

---

#### Dragon Frontiers
*TCG source: EX Dragon Frontiers (Japanese: さいはての攻防 — Offense and Defense of the Furthest Ends)*

Two islands "beyond the horizon": a rocky lower island and a Floating Island above it, encircled by a ring of clouds. The lower island has little plant life and few beaches — rocky coastline, a valley of staggered red sandstone cliffs and pillar formations, sparse scrub, a pine forest, and a swamp on the bank of an inland body of water. The Floating Island's surface is so high that the atmosphere transitions to a dark, starry outer-space void even in the middle of the day.

This is not an artistic effect. Dragon Frontiers sits at the point of greatest cosmic exposure in the region — where the planetary magnetic shielding is thinnest, where the boundary between atmosphere and space is most directly felt. This is precisely why the ancient civilization built their farthest and most significant site here. The crystal network's observatory function reads most clearly at Dragon Frontiers because the signal from beyond the atmosphere encounters least interference. The civilization's deepest records — their cosmic layer of knowledge — are here because this is the one location where what they were describing could be directly observed and confirmed.

The only known human resident of the lower island is an old hermit — see IV.5. Ancient structures: the second half of the civilization's paired diamond carving system (matched to Mirage Forest across an ocean), the **Holon Legacy** — three tall standing stones in a triangle around a shorter central stone, the Japanese name translating as *Holon's Holy Site* — and the **Sunken Stone**, a massive worked block cut at right angles, half-submerged in the sea.

**Key TCG lore:** The final Delta Species set. Nearly all Pokémon are Delta at full evolution, and none gain a Metal secondary type — the only location in the arc where this is true. The phenomenon here is naturalized, not imposed. The headline Gold Stars are Charizard ☆ δ (Darkness) and Mew ☆ δ (Water). Characters appearing here: Island Hermit, Professor Elm, Professor Oak, Mr. Stone.

**Card-level visual reference:**
- *The arid red cliffs:* Nidoking δ and Trapinch exist in massive jagged canyons of deep reddish-orange sedimentary rock. Typhlosion δ roars atop active magma fissures.
- *The ancient monoliths:* The Island Hermit stands beside beautifully carved ancient stone monoliths resting in shallow water.
- *The Floating Sky Island:* Altaria ex δ and Togetic δ — jagged rock spires piercing through a sea of white clouds.
- *The celestial edge:* Rayquaza ex δ and Ledian δ backgrounds transition into a dark starry outer-space sky in the middle of the day — the literal boundary of the known world.
- *The final discovery:* Mew ☆ δ swims peacefully in a pristine crystal-clear underwater sea.

**Delta behavior:** 100% of fully evolved Pokémon are Delta Species. Zero Metal secondary anywhere — the only location in the arc where this is true. These Delta have genuinely speciated — adapted, stable, occupying ecological niches the way island fauna diverges from mainland populations.

### II.3 Holon Village as a Living Town

Holon Village is the player's recurring anchor across the entire game. It does not announce story progress — it reflects it. NPCs move, disappear, and return. Conversations change. The atmosphere shifts as the situation escalates. The submerged machinery beneath the lake becomes visible from the right vantage points. Researchers who were confident become guarded. The Tower's electrical storms become more frequent. The player notices these things before they understand them.

---

### II.4 The Holon League

Holon's Pokémon League is not Devon's institution. It grew alongside the expedition — shaped by the same period that built the Tower and expanded the village — but it belongs to the region, not the company. Its eight gym leaders hold local authority: field specialists, zone ecologists, trainers whose expertise is specific to the terrain they occupy. Some have formal or informal arrangements with Devon; others operate at deliberate arm's length.

For the player, the league is the structure that opens the region. Gym credentials are not Devon clearance — they are recognition from the people who actually know each zone, earned through demonstrated competence in their terrain. Auren holds the Champion title, in a position Devon cannot appoint or revoke.

## Part III — The Delta Phenomenon

### III.1 What Delta Species Are

Delta Species are Pokémon whose type and biological profile have been altered by the electromagnetic field produced by the Holon Research Tower interacting with the regional crystal network and the field generated by Deoxys beneath the region. The phenomenon is a side effect the research team discovered without understanding they caused it.

**Tower-fingerprint rule:** The Metal secondary type is the Tower's specific signature. It appears wherever the Tower's broadcast is the primary cause and fades where the crystal network or long-term naturalization is the dominant force. Dragon Frontiers, furthest from the Tower and longest exposed, carries none of it.

**Tower-imposed vs. naturalized Delta:** Tower-caused Delta is a change in the environment that organisms have adapted to over years of continuous broadcast — stable but externally imposed. Dragon Frontiers Delta is something closer to genuine biological divergence: the phenomenon has been present long enough that it has become the baseline. The game's sharpest ecological argument sits in this distinction — adaptation driven by time and environment is life finding a way; transformation imposed by an external source on a compressed timeline is something else entirely.

---

### III.2 Holon's Pokémon & Holon Energy

Certain Pokémon near the Tower — the Castform, Electrode, and Magneton lines primarily — exhibit an anomalous property: they are capable of converting their own biology into pure energy, functioning as living batteries. Devon researchers name these **Holon's Pokémon**.

The discovery is made early in the expedition but takes years to fully understand or replicate. Through sustained study of Holon's Pokémon alongside Delta Species, Devon researchers eventually synthesize three new energy types: **Holon Energy FF, GL, and WP**. These are genuine scientific achievements — stable, reproducible, and with real applications for Pokémon training and field research.

The same principle that makes Holon's Pokémon remarkable — biological energy conversion — is what makes them exploitable. The distinction between a Holon's Pokémon functioning as a natural curiosity in a research context and one used as a forced energy source for industrial extraction is the line Epsilon crossed, and the line Ty eventually crosses behind them.

**δ Energy:** Delta Pokémon produce and conduct energy in ways normal Pokémon do not. δ Energy itself operates on a different principle than standard Pokémon energy — the full implications of this for the world outside Holon are an open development question.

---

### III.3 Devon's Poké Ball Technology

Two Poké Ball variants developed as a direct consequence of the Delta phenomenon:

**D Balls (Delta Balls):** Developed by Devon's public research program in response to the Delta phenomenon — Delta Species' type-shifted biology makes standard Poké Ball containment unreliable. D Balls are designed to capture and stabilize Delta Species specifically. Legitimate technology, openly discussed, a direct and good-faith product of Devon's scientific curiosity. The player receives them as field equipment.

**E Balls (Epsilon Balls):** Developed covertly by Epsilon after the Tower incident revealed Deoxys's existence, drawing on understanding of Holon's Pokémon and the Holon Energy synthesis. Outwardly similar to D Balls. Internally built around a different principle entirely: sustained energy conversion rather than stable containment. An E Ball holds a captured Pokémon in a state of active biological energy conversion — drawing on the Holon's Pokémon principle scaled to a much more powerful organism. The intended use is to capture Delta Deoxys and detonate its converted energy through the crystal network at Dragon Frontiers. [TBD: full technical mechanism.]

The D Ball and E Ball are the same technology at different stages of intent. Devon invented the principle in good faith. Epsilon weaponized it.

## Part V — Narrative Framework

### V.1 The Crichton/Crouch Commitment

The story operates in the tradition of Michael Crichton and Blake Crouch — not as stylistic homage but as a structural commitment.

**The disaster is never caused by evil.** Every person in this story is operating from a coherent, internally justified position. The Epsilon leader's diagnosis of the natural world is not wrong. Cozmo genuinely believes in the expedition's science. Steven genuinely believes in his organization. Ty genuinely believes the ecological crisis demands action now. Rayquaza's aggression is ecologically appropriate. None of them are villains. The catastrophe is a consequence of every system operating correctly at its own scale while remaining blind to the scale above it.

**The warning signs are always present.** The Metal typing on Tower-area Delta is evidence of a corrupted magnetic field — visible to anyone who knew what to look for. The ancient civilization's sites are evidence that someone understood what was buried there. The anomalous Delta behavior at Holon Lake is evidence that something other than the Tower is causing it. The Castaway's readings are evidence that something is building. The aurora surge is the same evidence made visible to the naked eye, misread at first. Every piece of the picture is in the world. The player assembles it at the same rate it becomes too late to use cleanly.

**Expertise is the blind spot.** Every character's failure is a direct consequence of what makes them good at their specific job. They are not missing information because they are incompetent — they are missing it because they are only looking at the part of the system their expertise covers.

**The slow burn with moments of sharp action.** The dread accumulates quietly. Strange ecological readings. A researcher nervous about the wrong things. Then something breaks open. Then quiet again, but the quiet is different now. The most frightening moments land hardest after silence.

---

### V.2 Thematic Spine

**Thesis:** Nature answers intrusion with force of its own — not to restore what was, but to find where it can stand again. The only way to meet that honestly is to keep looking instead of deciding you already understand.

**Primary themes — dynamical:**

- **Scale and blindness** — every system in this story is complete and correct within its own frame. The disaster is not caused by failure, malice, or incompetence. It is the structural consequence of agents operating at their own resolution, unable to perceive the scale above them. Every whole thing is also a part; forgetting that is how disasters start, and remembering it is the only way to end one.

- **Sensitivity and emergence** — small perturbations in coupled systems produce disproportionate, unpredictable effects. None of the causes in this story contain their consequences. The same interconnection that produces catastrophe produces the capacity to resolve it. The theme asks the player to hold both truths simultaneously.

- **Equilibrium and reaction** — every technological intrusion into a living system provokes a countervailing correction, proportional not to the intrusion's intent but to its scale. Rayquaza's aggression, Deoxys's destabilization, the crystal network's amplification, the sun's own indifferent cycle converging on the exact moment humanity weakened the shield against it — none of these are malice. They are a system finding where it can stand again. Critically, the correction does not return the system to its prior state. It settles into a new equilibrium — colder, stranger, and permanently different from the one the intrusion disturbed. This is the mechanism behind the game's refusal of a restoration ending: the world after is not fixed, it is resettled. The emotional counterpart of that refusal — how the characters and the player come to live in a world that will not revert — is developed below under *Acceptance vs. restoration*.

**Major emotional theme:**

- **Curiosity vs. judgment** — judgment forecloses inquiry the moment it decides it already understands; curiosity keeps looking past that point. Mew came back to the power spot because it has never been able to stay away from the only thing on Earth it cannot understand, and that refusal to stop looking is what wakes Deoxys and transforms Mew itself. The researchers came to Holon to find Mew and found something that reorganized everything they understood about Pokémon biology. The player follows the same thread across five zones and arrives somewhere none of the characters who came before them reached — not because they are stronger, but because they never stopped asking the next question. The emotional register is not triumph or tragedy. It is the particular feeling of having followed something true all the way to where it leads, and finding that the journey changed you as much as it changed the destination.

- **Acceptance vs. restoration** — every Pokémon story trains its player to expect the ending where the threat is beaten and the world returns to normal. This game withholds it. The Delta phenomenon is permanent. Mew does not change back. Delta Mewtwo stays in the lake as evidence no one can remove. Deoxys is restored, but the region it re-stabilizes is colder, stranger, and permanently unlike the one it left. Every character embodies a different relationship to that irreversibility. Steven stops waiting for Devon to be the company he believed in and steps into the one that exists. Mr. Stone stops sheltering behind not having known. The Castaway keeps his post after everyone else has gone home, then moves further out rather than back. Auren left Devon but not Holon — a changed attachment, not a severed one. The ancient civilization is simply gone, and the player never learns why; some of what is lost stays lost and unreadable, and the game lets it. Ty is the counter-figure: underneath the ecological argument, his urgency is a refusal to grieve — he cannot accept a world that asks him to wait. The Epsilon leader is that refusal fully hardened: an attempt to force the world back to a healthier state by transforming everything in it at once. The player's position is neither Ty's nor mere resignation — it is the work of grieving what is gone, protecting what can still be kept, and finding, as the thesis puts it, where the world can stand again. The game's own ecological distinction carries the same meaning at another scale: naturalized Delta is life adapting over time; Tower-imposed Delta is change inflicted too fast to absorb. Change integrated is survival. Change forced is violence.

**The player/Ty divergence as thematic expression:**

- Curiosity vs. judgment is the human-scale expression of the game's primary themes. Both the player and Ty encounter the same evidence. The player's response is to keep looking — more questions, more context, more picture. Ty's response is to render a verdict on what's already known — the evidence is sufficient, hesitation is complicity, further inquiry is just delay. Neither instinct is wrong in isolation; judgment is what lets anyone act at all. What the game asks is whether the curiosity that drives the player is the same thing that makes them capable of assembling the full picture before it's too late — and whether Ty's judgment, which is urgency mistaken for clarity, is the thing that makes him dangerous. The Epsilon leader is Ty's endpoint: a man who stopped investigating his own conclusions a long time ago.

- The same divergence runs through acceptance vs. restoration. The player learns to live in a world that will not revert; Ty cannot, and reads every call for patience as complicity. Curiosity and acceptance are the same disposition in two registers — epistemic and emotional — the willingness to stay with something unfinished. Judgment and restoration are their matched failures: both are attempts to be done, one with inquiry and one with grief. This is why Ty and the Epsilon leader converge: they arrive at the same act from the same inability to wait.

**Minor themes — human and ethical:**

- **Scientific curiosity vs. restraint and unintended consequences** — every hand that reshaped something in this story believed it was helping. The ethical weight is not in the intention but in the failure to account for what a coupled system does with an intervention. Devon's fossil restoration program is wonder. The same program pointed at apex predators to use as weapons is catastrophe. The technology is identical. The intent is everything, and intent is not enough.

- **Human effect on ecosystems and the natural order** — the Delta phenomenon is permanent. The ending is not a restoration. It is a survival, and the world after it is genuinely different from the world before — the new equilibrium the region settles into, not the one it left.

---

### V.3 Systems Within Systems

The holon principle is not only a narrative theme. It is the literal dynamical structure of the game's disaster.

**All systems in play:**

The sun — running its own cycle, indifferent to anything on Earth. Deoxys — a dormant extraterrestrial organism whose intact biology stabilizes the planetary magnetic field and, through the field's own directional structure, channels its energy into what becomes the crystal network. The crystal network — a region-spanning EM infrastructure and planetary observatory, grown wherever that channeling concentrated, tended by the ancient civilization. Devon's public research program — legitimate science operating above the power spot without knowing what's there. Devon's fossil restoration program — genuine ecological achievement, and the same technology Epsilon repurposed as a weapon. Devon's underwater infrastructure — built at the same points of field concentration by ordinary engineering logic, unknowingly tied into the network. Epsilon's expansion of that infrastructure — accidentally constructing the most sensitive EM observatory on Earth. The Volcano Observatory — made dramatically more sensitive by the expansion, reading a signal nobody intended to find. The aurora — the same signal made visible without instruments, misread at first as Deoxys's doing. Rayquaza — a territorial guardian responding correctly to the largest ecological disruption its territory has experienced. Mew — drawn back by the same ancient instinct that brought it the first time, now transformed. The player's own science team — the only agents whose collaborative synthesis crosses every other system's boundary.

**Nested structure — scale hierarchy:**

- The sun's natural cycle
    - The planetary magnetic field
        - Deoxys as regional and planetary magnetic stabilizer
            - The crystal network as regional EM infrastructure and observatory, grown where the field's geometry concentrates Deoxys's energy
                - The ancient civilization's sites at those same points of concentration
                    - Devon's underwater infrastructure unknowingly built at the same points, chasing signal strength rather than understanding why it was strong there
                        - Epsilon's expansion seeding new nodes
                            - The Volcano Observatory reading the expanded network's signal
                            - The aurora surge, the same signal read with the naked eye
                - The power spot as the network's origin point
                    - The Tower broadcasting into the power spot
                        - The Tower's field amplifying what was already there
                            - Delta Species across the region
                                - The player and their science team assembling the full picture across all five zones

## Era 5 — The Incident

- The Tower is pushed beyond safe operating parameters during one of Epsilon's escalations. The result is a massive burst of EM Delta radiation — not just into the atmosphere, but downward, through the Power Spot, into the ground.
- The burst reaches Deoxys, dormant beneath the facility. Deoxys wakes — inside a magnetic environment completely unlike the one it went dormant in. The Tower's field has been running for years, corrupting the regional magnetic baseline. Deoxys wakes into that corruption and its biology reacts: it shifts into a **Delta Deoxys** state, its forme-cycling mechanism expressing through a contaminated field it has no framework for.
- Deoxys breaks through the earth and up through the Tower facility. The ensuing explosion destroys part of the Tower and devastates the surrounding area. Devon covers it up — officially an equipment malfunction, the Tower shut down for repairs.
- The explosion also sends a pulse of Delta radiation outward through the crystal network, propagating across the region and beyond — into the wider world. [TBD: what this pulse does at a global scale, how it connects to the solar flare subplot — see Open Questions.]
- Deoxys escapes into the region. Its whereabouts become unknown — to Devon, to Epsilon, and to everyone else.
- This changes Epsilon's plans fundamentally. They now know Deoxys exists — an entity with the ability to rewrite its own DNA in real time, the ultimate expression of everything they've been working toward. Capturing it becomes the new priority. But the technology to do what they're imagining doesn't exist yet. Epsilon begins developing it in the months that follow, in parallel with the Tower being repaired and brought back online.

- What is the specific nature of the ancient civilization's belief about the "guardian" — how developed is their cosmology, what does their architecture communicate about their values beyond "build around power, not through it," and how much of this is legible to the player through the ruins versus permanently illegible?
- Did the ancient civilization have a name for themselves or for Deoxys?
- What caused the ancient civilization to disappear, and is this ever revealed?
- Does the outward radiation pulse from the Tower incident have visible effects on the wider world that the player encounters as evidence?
- What is the specific mechanism of the Act 4 aurora correction — witnessed directly, explained by an NPC, or pieced together by the player across sightings?

**Characters**
- What is the Epsilon leader's name? What is his arc after Ty defeats him at Crystal Isles? What is the specific sequence of his radicalization?
- How does Steven know the player, and what specifically made them the right choice to invite?
- What is Ty's region of origin and background? What does he receive as a starter, and how does that choice reflect his character?
- What is the specific incident in Act 4 that marks Ty's visible divergence from the player? Does he join Epsilon formally, or pursue the same ends through his own logic without joining them?
- Where does Ty end up at the end? Is there reconciliation, permanent divergence, or something between?
- Does Mr. Stone arrive at Dragon Frontiers during the main story or postgame, and what does he know?
- What is the mechanism by which Steven becomes Devon's president — does Mr. Stone step down willingly, is it a generational handoff already in motion, does the scandal force it? What does Steven's Devon look like differently from his father's?
- What did Auren actually know and when? Why did they stay in Holon rather than leave the region entirely? What is their relationship, if any, to Cozmo, Steven, or the Epsilon leader? Do they play any active role in the main story before the postgame Champion match?
- When and how does the Island Hermit identity connect to the Castaway throughline — is the Hermit who the Castaway becomes after leaving Crystal Isles, does the player encounter the Hermit before or after putting the connection together, what drove the move, and what does the reveal look like in play?

**Technology**
- What is the exact mechanism of E Ball conversion — how does it hold Deoxys in a sustained energy-conversion state?
- What is the modified D Ball mechanic used to revert Delta Deoxys — how does the player acquire or create it?
- How exactly do the Tower, the lake infrastructure, the crystal network, and the E/D Balls interact when combined — what does the emergent system actually do that none of them could do alone?
- Which legendary Pokémon does Epsilon attempt to resurrect to hunt Deoxys — Primal Groudon, Kyogre, both, or something else? What is the exact staging of the now-witnessed event — does the player arrive in time to see the resurrection itself, arrive as it's already destabilizing, or arrive specifically for the breakout and scattering?
- What is the Holon Transceiver's function in this continuity — a language-reading tool for the ancient ruins, something requiring a harvested crystal, Devon technology, ancient technology, or something the player finds independently? How does it relate, if at all, to the D Ball/E Ball lineage?

**The Rival & Finale**
- What is the battle sequence at the Floating Island — how does the player stop the E Ball from detonating?
- Does Rayquaza need to be caught, or is the alliance formed another way? Does the player encounter Rayquaza's Delta forme during the main story or only postgame? Are multiple Rayquaza sightings across the game the same individual or separate appearances until the climax?
- Is Deoxys catchable in the postgame after being restored?
- How does the curiosity vs. judgment framing play out concretely across the game — through dialogue choices, through specific story beats, through how each character treats Pokémon and people they don't yet understand?

**The Solar Crisis**
- What is the Castaway's final transmission format — cutscene, message received mid-travel, or something read in the observatory on a return visit?
- How explicit is the comet mention at the first observatory visit as a hint toward the Jirachi postgame — does it read as foreshadowing in retrospect, or is it buried enough that only a second playthrough catches it?

**Gym Leaders & League**
- Who are the eight gym leaders — names, types, locations, and personalities? Which acts and zones do they belong to? What does each reveal about Devon or the region through dialogue, and what is each leader's individual relationship to Devon?

**Postgame Content**
- What origin, location, or connection (if any) does Shadow Lugia/XD001 have to the Epsilon/Deoxys plotline?
- Does Delta Horizon's unlock condition tie to defeating Auren, to the Dragon Frontiers resolution, or to something else? Is any Delta Horizon resident connected to Ty, the Castaway, or another named character? Does the island have any relationship to the ancient civilization? Does HL-500's "outward pulse" reference the same Tower incident established here, or does this need reconciling?
