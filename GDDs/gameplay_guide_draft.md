# Pokémon Holon Legends — Gameplay & Region Guide

---

## Design Philosophy

This game is built on the structure and feel of Gen 3 Pokémon — Ruby, Sapphire, Emerald. Eight gyms, a Pokémon League, HMs, a professor, a Pokédex. The player moves forward through a region that rewards curiosity without announcing rewards. There is no mission menu. There are no quest markers. Story events, world discoveries, and character moments are woven into the environment and triggered by the player moving through it, the same way the original games worked — and the same way games like Elden Ring work at a higher fidelity. The world is full of things that respond to the player's progress without ever calling attention to themselves as tasks.

The five canonical TCG locations — Mirage Forest, Holon Village, Holon Lake, the Unnamed Island, Dragon Frontiers — are the story anchors. Everything between them is connective tissue: towns, routes, landmarks, and people that make the region feel inhabited. The total location count expands well beyond five to support 8 gyms, a full route network, and a region that feels like it existed before the player arrived.

---

## Region Overview

The Holon region is divided into three geographic zones that the player moves through in sequence:

**The Mainland** — the forested interior where the game begins. Entrance Town, Mirage Forest, Holon Village, Holon Lake, and the routes and settlements between them. The Tower's Delta influence is felt here. Five gyms sit on the mainland.

**The Archipelago** — a chain of islands reached by boat from the mainland coast. The Unnamed Island is the largest, but the chain includes inhabited islands, research outposts, and the satellite islets. Three gyms sit in the archipelago.

**Dragon Frontiers** — the furthest point. Two islands beyond the horizon: the lower island and the floating island above it. The Pokémon League sits here. No gyms — this is endgame territory.

## Full Location List

The following is every named location in the region in player-progression order. Canonical TCG locations are marked. Invented connective locations are marked. All are consistent with the lore bible.

---

### Zone 1 — The Mainland

#### Entrance Town *(invented)*
The player's starting location. A modern town at the edge of Mirage Forest — stone buildings, paved streets, electrical lighting. The last ordinary place before the forest starts. A professor operates here; the player receives their starter and Pokédex. The forest road north leads into Mirage Forest proper. Routes east and west lead to the first gym town.

- **Professor:** Based here. Sends the player into the region to document the Delta phenomenon. The framing is scientific — the player is a new research assistant, not a chosen hero.
- **Starter Pokémon:** Three Delta Species. Choices TBD.
- **Pokédex:** Issued here. Framed as a research tool rather than a personal goal, though completion is tracked and rewarded.

#### Route 1 — Forest Edge
The introductory route. Light forest, low-level wild Pokémon, gentle tutorial pacing. No Delta Pokémon yet — this is unaffected forest, before the Tower's signal reaches. The absence of Delta here is itself a contrast the player will notice later.

#### Mirage Forest — Outer *(TCG: EX Legend Maker)*
The forest begins in earnest. Dense canopy, Jurassic-era flora, exposed rock, clearings. The first Delta Pokémon appear here — distant enough from the Tower that changes are subtle, mostly in coloring and minor type shifts. The forest feels ancient and slightly wrong in a way the player can't immediately name.

Notable sub-areas accessible progressively:
- The Railway (early — visible but impassable without context)
- The Cursed Shrine (mid-game revisit)
- Strange Cave (mid-game revisit)
- Full Flame Clearing (available but mysterious)
- The Power Tree Clearing (late game)
- Sand Basin (requires HM)
- Ice Plateau (requires HM)
- Regi Chambers (postgame)

#### Mirage Forest Town *(invented)*
A small settlement inside the forest — researchers, a few families, people who have been here longer than the current team and remember what the forest was like before the Tower was built. One gym here.

**Gym 1** — the player's first battle. Delta Pokémon are the theme but the leader's team reflects early, subtle mutations — mostly normal Pokémon with one or two Delta variants. Low difficulty. The leader is a botanist who has been documenting how the forest's Pokémon have changed over the years.

#### Route 2 — Deep Forest
Longer route, more varied terrain. Delta presence increases noticeably. The player starts to see consistent type changes in wild Pokémon. A fork in the road — one path continues toward Holon, another leads toward a dead end that will open later with an HM.

#### Holon Village *(TCG: EX Delta Species)*
The central hub of the region and the game's primary recurring anchor. The Poké Ball layout, canals, farms, the observatory building, the research team. The Holon Research Tower stands at the absolute center, cracked and quiet since the accident. 

The player arrives here at the end of Act 1. The accident has already happened. The researchers are in various states of alarm, denial, and action. The faction is present but not yet revealed as adversarial — they appear to be part of the research effort.

**Gym 2** — located in Holon Village. The leader is a senior researcher whose team is all Tower-adjacent Delta Pokémon — Fire/Metal and Lightning/Metal types, the Tower's specific fingerprint. Beating this gym grants the player authorization to travel south toward Holon Lake.

The Tower itself is not accessible to the player at this point. Its basement — where the original stone is buried — is sealed. This becomes relevant at the ending.

**First Steven appearance:** He is somewhere in the village when the player arrives. He has already been here for a while. He says something about the Holon Ruins that doesn't fully make sense yet. He leaves.

#### Holon Ruins *(TCG: EX Delta Species)*
An abandoned building in the Holon architectural style, elsewhere in the village. The researchers know it's there. Steven is the one who eventually understands what it is. Accessible from early in the game but its significance isn't clear until much later. Contains environmental details that reward a return visit after the mid-game.

#### Route 3 — Riverside
The river running past Holon village's exterior becomes the route south. Riverside terrain, water-adjacent wild Pokémon, some Delta. Leads toward the lake region. A branch route leads east toward a small settlement and the third gym.

#### Lakeside Town *(invented)*
A town on the northern edge of the Holon Lake area. Fishing community, researchers who arrived before the accident and stayed. The Delta mutations here are already noticeably different from Holon's Tower-caused changes — the lake's patterns are anomalous. People here are uneasy but haven't left.

**Gym 3** — the leader has been studying the lake's anomalous Delta patterns and has built a team that reflects them. Water and Metal types with unusual combinations. Mid-difficulty.

#### Route 4 — Fern Lowlands *(TCG: Meowth card, POP Series 5)*
Dense fern undergrowth. The transition from the river terrain to the lake basin. Wild Pokémon here are almost all Delta. The ecosystem disruption is visible — Pokémon that shouldn't be sharing territory are, because their type relationships have been scrambled. A researcher NPC standing in the ferns mentions that the predator-prey balance is completely wrong and getting worse.

#### Holon Lake *(TCG: EX Holon Phantoms)*
The lake itself. The large lake, the jagged blue mountain range, the marshland. Defense δ is somewhere in this area — barely moving, field growing. The player doesn't encounter it directly yet; they see its effects.

The **Stone Dome Grove** is here — discovered by the player by exploring, not by being directed. An adventurer NPC found nearby has been mapping the domes for weeks. Steven has already been here and left a mark — a sketch pinned to one of the domes, saying nothing that makes complete sense yet.

The **Lake Structure** — three pillars, central sphere — is visible from the shore. It is active. The researchers don't know why. The faction has been observing it.

**Second Steven appearance:** At the Stone Dome Grove. He is studying the carvings. He tells the player the geometric patterns match something he has seen in the Holon Ruins. Then he says the carvings are older than the Ruins by a significant margin. He leaves before explaining what that means.

#### Stone Spike Badlands *(TCG: Anorith card)*
The far end of the mountain range, where it breaks into isolated stone columns. A dramatic sub-area accessible from the lake's far shore. No gym here, but a significant environmental discovery — diamond carvings matching those in Mirage Forest, here at the lake, separated by the entire mainland. Background lore. The player notices them. Nobody explains them.

#### Marshland Settlement *(invented)*
A small research outpost on the marshland side of the lake. The faction has a visible presence here that makes the player slightly uncomfortable — they're here "to help," but their equipment is pointed at the Lake Structure rather than away from it. One of the faction's researchers will say something that doesn't add up if the player talks to them.

#### Route 5 — Mountain Pass
The route south from the lake region, through the lower end of the mountain range. Elevation change, different wild Pokémon, some cave sections. The Delta mutations change character noticeably — the transition zone between Tower-caused and fragment-caused phenomena. A researcher on this route has been documenting the change and is confused by the discontinuity.

#### Coastal Town *(invented)*
The first proper port town. A real settlement — not a research outpost, a community. Fishing, trade, some tourism before the accident made things complicated. The Delta phenomenon hasn't affected this town as severely because it's at the edge of the Tower's range. The people here are aware of what's happening inland but at a remove. A sailor will eventually take the player to the archipelago after the fourth gym is cleared.

**Gym 4** — the leader is a sailor and naturalist who has been observing the Delta phenomenon's coastal effects. Normal/Water types, some Delta. The gym's aesthetic reflects the port setting. Clearing this gym opens sea travel.

**Gym 5** — also on the mainland, in a town between Coastal Town and the route to the League. This location is TBD in terms of exact placement but sits logically between the coast and the mountain interior. The leader and theme are TBD pending overall gym distribution decisions.

### Zone 2 — The Archipelago

#### Route 6 — Open Sea
The boat route between the mainland and the archipelago. Not a traditional route — the player travels by ship. An NPC on the boat comments on the ocean. A faction researcher is also on board, traveling in the same direction, which the player may or may not find significant depending on timing.

#### Outer Island Town *(invented)*
The first inhabited island in the archipelago chain — a small but proper town, not a research outpost. The Delta phenomenon is present here but mild. The people here are aware of the mainland crisis in the way islanders are aware of mainland problems: at a distance, with some skepticism about how serious it actually is. This changes over the course of the game.

**Gym 6** — an island-native trainer who specializes in Pokémon that have always lived on these islands, some of which have their own natural Delta variations that predate the accident. The distinction between Tower-caused and naturally-occurring Delta starts to become meaningful here.

#### Route 7 — Island Chain
The route between the outer islands and the Unnamed Island. Multiple small islands. The satellite islets are here — visible from the boat, explorable on foot with the right HM. Nothing announces what's on them. They reward exploration.

#### Research Outpost Island *(invented)*
A mid-chain island that functions as a staging point for researchers traveling to the Unnamed Island. The faction has a substantial presence here and is less careful about hiding their agenda at this distance from the mainland. The player can overhear things. The faction's lead researcher may appear here briefly — not as a confrontation, just as a presence the player registers.

#### The Unnamed Island *(TCG: EX Crystal Guardians)*
Tropical, volcanic, covered in glowing crystals. The largest island in the archipelago. Attack δ is here — reactive, dangerous, the source of most of the collateral damage Rayquaza is responding to. The player doesn't go straight to Attack δ; they work through the island's environments first, building toward the encounter.

The castaway sailor on the island's accessible beach has been here long enough to know things no researcher does. He talks about the crystals, about the Holon Circle, about something he saw in the caves that he can't explain and doesn't particularly want to. He gives the player information without framing it as a clue.

**Gym 7** — on the Unnamed Island. The leader is a researcher who arrived before the accident, got stranded when travel became complicated, and has been studying the island in isolation since. Their team is entirely island-native Delta Pokémon — no Metal secondary type, which is the first time the player encounters this and may not immediately understand what it means. High difficulty.

The **Crystal Caves** are accessible after Gym 7. The **Holon Circle** — three crystal pillars in a crater — is here. The faction is investigating it and not happy to see the player.

**Attack δ encounter** — deep in the island's interior, past the Crystal Caves. The faction is also trying to reach it. The player gets there first or at the same time. The encounter with Attack δ is the most contested fragment encounter in the game.

**Third Steven appearance:** Somewhere on the Unnamed Island, after the Attack δ encounter. He has been here. He has found something in the Crystal Caves that he is holding onto. He tells the player something about the pre-accident Tower logs — not the full picture, just that the readings don't match what the faction says caused it. He says he needs more data. He leaves.

### Zone 3 — Dragon Frontiers

#### Route 8 — Deep Ocean
The long boat crossing to Dragon Frontiers. The furthest the player has traveled from Holon. Weather changes. The sea looks different. An NPC on the boat says they've made this crossing before and something is different this time — the water Pokémon are behaving strangely.

#### Dragon Frontiers — Lower Island *(TCG: EX Dragon Frontiers)*
The lower island. Rocky coastline, Red Cliff Valley, the coastal pine forest, Dragon Frontiers Swamp, Holon Legacy on the beach. Nearly everything here is Delta — no Metal, fully natural. The player has been building toward understanding what this means.

The **Hermit's Settlement** is on the pine coast. The hermit has been here for years. He has seen Speed δ. He describes it in a way that makes clear he doesn't know what it is, only what it does — never in the same place twice, the area around it buzzing. He tells the player where it was last.

Speed δ is encountered here — on the lower island, erratic and evasive. The encounter is the most difficult fragment encounter because Speed δ doesn't stay still. This is the last fragment.

The **Holon Legacy** — stone circle on the only beach — is here. The player can find it simply by exploring the coastline. It matches the pattern of every other three-node structure in the region. Nobody announces this. The player notices or doesn't.

**Gym 8** — on the lower island. The leader is the only permanent human resident other than the hermit — a Dragon-type specialist who has been studying the natural Delta phenomenon here for years and has the deepest understanding of any gym leader of what Delta actually is vs. what the Tower caused. Very high difficulty. Their team is entirely natural Dragon δ Pokémon with no Metal secondary.

After Gym 8, the path to Rayquaza opens. Rayquaza is above — circling the floating island, blocking access.

**Rayquaza encounter** — the player meets Rayquaza on the lower island's highest point. This is not a straightforward battle. The exact mechanic is TBD but the resolution clears the path to the floating island.

#### The Pokémon League *(invented — lower island)*
Built on the lower island, near the base of the floating island's shadow. The League exists here because Dragon Frontiers is the administrative endpoint of authorized Holon research access — getting here required clearance at every previous checkpoint. The Elite Four are the four most experienced researchers and field specialists in the region, each reflecting one of the four Delta phenomenon types. Steven is the Champion.

**Steven's pre-endgame scene** happens here, after the player defeats him. He tells the player what he found — the Tower's pre-accident logs, the second signal in the data, what it means. He says what Mew did. He says it quietly. He says he needed to be sure before saying it. Now the player knows everything. The floating island is above them.

#### The Floating Island *(TCG: EX Dragon Frontiers — surface undefined)*
The endgame area. The surface of the floating island is never depicted in the TCG — what is up there is for the game to define. The faction is already there or arrives at the same time. The final faction battle happens here. The four fragments converge. Mew ☆ δ appears and shows the fragments the way back.

The player then returns to Holon for the ending.

---

### The Ending — Holon Research Tower Basement

The four fragments have gone ahead of the player, drawn back by the signal Mew showed them. The player descends beneath the Tower for the first time — the basement that was always sealed, where the organic architecture is strangest. The original stone is there. The fragments are gathered around it.

The four fractured Deoxys δ encounter happens here. After the restoration, the player has the opportunity to catch Deoxys — whole, restored, at the place where it all began.

## Discovery Events

The following are things that exist in the world and respond to player exploration without ever being announced. They are the Elden Ring layer of the game — present for players who look, invisible to players who don't, never mandatory.

**The Railway** — a section of disused rail track in Mirage Forest. No origin or destination visible. A researcher in Entrance Town will mention it if the player asks about the forest's history. Steven mentions it in passing in his first appearance. No further explanation is ever given.

**The diamond carvings** — found in Mirage Forest, again at the Stone Spike Badlands near Holon Lake, and again at Dragon Frontiers. Each instance is just there. A researcher NPC near the second instance will note that the carvings match ones described in old survey records from Mirage Forest. The third instance — at Dragon Frontiers, across an ocean — has no NPC commentary at all.

**The three-node pattern** — the Tower's satellite structures, the Lake Structure, Holon Circle, Holon Legacy. Each exists as a location feature. No NPC connects them explicitly. A player paying attention will notice the pattern themselves. This is never stated as significant in dialogue.

**The Holon Ruins interior** — accessible early, strange architecture. The organic walls and glowing machinery. Researchers shrug it off. Steven's note in his first appearance, reread after his second appearance, suddenly has a different meaning.

**Mew's trail** — after the ending, ecological traces of a Water-type Delta Mew moving through each location. No pointer. No notification. The player finds them or doesn't. Leads eventually to Mirage Forest and the Mew ☆ δ postgame encounter.

**The Sunken Stone** — half-submerged worked stone off the Dragon Frontiers coast. Visible from the shore. Surf-accessible. Just there. No explanation. Connects to the ancient civilization in a way nobody in the game ever says aloud.

**Ampharos δ** — somewhere in the region, an Ampharos δ whose Holon Veil effect is somehow active. The player encounters an area where all Pokémon temporarily read as Delta regardless of what they are. A researcher in the area is baffled and taking notes. No explanation is given in dialogue. Players who understand the TCG mechanic will recognize what's happening.

## Pokédex

The Pokédex is framed as a research tool issued by the professor. Completion is tracked and rewarded at the professor's lab. The regional Pokédex contains standard Gen 3 Pokémon plus their Delta variants — each Delta Species has its own separate entry, reflecting the research team's documentation of the phenomenon.

Delta variants are catalogued separately from their base species. Completing the Delta section of the Pokédex — documenting every Delta form — is a distinct goal from completing the base Pokédex and is treated as the scientific achievement it would actually be within the world. The professor responds differently to Delta completion than to base completion.

## Postgame Structure

After the credits, the following becomes available without announcement. The player returns to a region that has settled — quieter, the EM field fading, the ecosystem in a new equilibrium.

**Accessible immediately:**
- Floating island surface — now reachable from above. What is up there is TBD.
- Regi chambers — three chambers in the Mirage Forest ruins. Steven has left a note at each. The player opens them or doesn't.
- Full satellite islet exploration — previously limited by sea conditions.

**Accessible through exploration:**
- Groudon ☆ and Kyogre ☆ — somewhere in the deep mainland wilderness. Non-Delta. No announcement.
- Mewtwo δ — Holon Lake, still present.
- Celebi ☆ and Alakazam ☆ — Unnamed Island, in areas that weren't fully accessible during the main story.
- Faction researchers — some remain. Their dialogue has changed. Some are remorseful. Some aren't.

**The Mew ☆ δ mission:**
Traces of Mew's passage through each of the five locations, woven into the environment. One per location. The trail requires attention — changed Pokémon behavior, a disturbed clearing, something slightly wrong about the water near a shore. Leads back to Mirage Forest. The final encounter is there. No fanfare. Just Mew, in the place where everything started, catchable at last.

## Open Questions for This Document

The following are not yet decided and should be addressed in subsequent design sessions:

- **Exact placement of Gym 5** — the fifth mainland gym needs a town and a leader identity
- **Rival** — whether one exists, their identity, when they appear
- **Faction name** — what the group calls itself in-universe
- **Gym leader identities and teams** — all eight need names, specialisms, and Delta-themed teams
- **Floating island surface** — what the endgame area looks like and what is up there
- **Rayquaza encounter mechanic** — how exactly this works
- **Deoxys encounter mechanic** — sequential battles, cycling formes, or another structure
- **Professor identity** — name, personality, relationship to the research team
- **HM move names** — whether standard Gen 3 HMs are used or renamed to fit the setting
- **Route count and numbering** — exact number of routes pending final map layout
