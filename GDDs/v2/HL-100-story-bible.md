## 1. Overview

A Pokémon ROM hack built on **pokeemerald expansion** (Pokémon Emerald decomp), set in the **Holon region** from the TCG Delta Species arc. The game tells the story of a scientific accident, an ancient mystery, and a living organism from space that has been dormant beneath a research tower for thousands of years.

The Holon Research Tower has been broadcasting electromagnetic energy for years, slowly and unintentionally transforming the Pokémon living around it into Delta Species — creatures with different elemental types than their species would normally carry. The researchers called it a discovery. Then something went wrong: the Tower's broadcast interacted with something buried beneath it, and what had been a contained local phenomenon became a spreading ecological crisis.

The player travels outward from Mirage Forest through five locations that mirror the five TCG sets of the Delta Species arc, piecing together what happened, what is still happening, and how to stop it before the region's ecosystems collapse entirely.

## 2. The World

### 2.1 Holon

A region built around a place — the power spot deep in Mirage Forest. Holon is named after the philosophical concept of a "holon": something that is simultaneously a whole and a part of something larger. The researchers who named it understood they were working with something that connected outward to forces they couldn't fully see.

The region spans five distinct locations, each corresponding to a phase of the Delta phenomenon's spread:

| Location | TCG Set | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mirage Forest | EX Legend Maker | Where it began; crash site; Holon is built here |
| Holon (Research Tower) | EX Delta Species | The Tower causes local Delta; the accident happens here |
| Holon Lake | EX Holon Phantoms | South of Holon; anomalous Delta patterns |
| Unnamed Island | EX Crystal Guardians | Remote; Delta least prevalent here |
| Dragon Frontiers | EX Dragon Frontiers | Furthest out; natural Delta; floating island above |

### 2.2 The Power Spot

Deep in Mirage Forest. Radiates mysterious healing energy. Holon was built on top of it. Before the researchers arrived, the ancient civilization treated it as sacred ground — built around it, documented it, never disturbed it.

**What it actually is:** The meteorite crash site. The residual extraterrestrial biological energy of the dormant Deoxys, leaking upward through centuries of rock and earth, is what manifests as the power spot's healing properties. Nobody in the current era knows this. The researchers studied the energy for years without identifying its source.

### 2.3 The Holon Research Tower

Built by the research team as an electromagnetic tracking instrument, specifically designed to locate Mew by detecting the unique EM signatures associated with Mew sightings in Mirage Forest lore. The Tower sits directly above the buried crash site — and the dormant Deoxys — without the researchers ever realizing it.

The Tower has been broadcasting continuously since construction. This ongoing broadcast is the direct cause of Delta Species mutations around Holon: years of electromagnetic saturation slowly rewiring the biology of local Pokémon. The Metal secondary typing, the restricted type changes to Fire and Lightning, the Holon's Pokémon that can transform into Energy — all of this is the Tower operating as designed, with unintended biological consequences accumulating over time. The researchers studied these mutations as a discovery. They did not understand they were the cause.

The Tower's interior has an unusual organic quality that doesn't match its exterior architecture. Glowing circular machinery. Features that seem less constructed than grown. Whether this is a design choice by the original builders, or something that developed gradually through proximity to what is buried beneath, is left open.

## 3. Deoxys

### 3.1 What Deoxys Is

A mutation of an extraterrestrial virus, exposed to a laser beam in space. Its name derives from DNA. It is not a Pokémon in the conventional sense — it evolved along an entirely different branch of life, one that has no evolutionary relatives anywhere on Earth.

Key properties:
- **While inactive, takes the form of a triangular stone tablet.** This is confirmed canon and central to the story.
- Communicates and perceives the world through aurora electromagnetic wavelengths.
- Disrupted and effectively blinded by EM interference from human technology.
- Can rewrite its own DNA in real time, cycling between four distinct formes — each with different stats, abilities, and behavioral tendencies.
- Can regenerate its entire body from its crystal brain alone.
- Can generate clones — duller in color, no crystal, locked in Normal Forme.
- Possibly a species rather than an individual. Multiple meteorites have been documented; multiple Deoxys are implied.

### 3.2 Deoxys's Four Formes

Forme-shifting is not a slow process or a conscious one in the way a human makes decisions. It is biological — closer to how a cuttlefish changes color than how a person changes clothes. Each forme represents a different optimization of the same underlying organism:

| Forme | Optimization | Behavioral tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | Balanced | Default state; most social, most communicative |
| Attack | Offense | Aggressive, reactive, threat-focused |
| Defense | Endurance | Closed off, static, oriented toward survival |
| Speed | Mobility | Erratic, evasive, in constant motion |

In a healthy Deoxys, these formes cycle deliberately in response to circumstances. In a fractured one, each fragment is locked into a single optimization with no ability to shift — the behavior becomes exaggerated, fixed, without the balance the other formes provided.

### 3.3 Deoxys and Rayquaza

Rayquaza attacks Deoxys on sight as a territorial invader — a canonical rivalry. Rayquaza patrols the upper atmosphere and treats anything descending from space as a threat. This isn't malice. It's territory.

In the context of this game: Rayquaza previously encountered whole, coherent Deoxys. What it encounters now are four fractured Delta versions doing things Deoxys has never done — spreading a phenomenon through its territory, locked in behavioral extremes, not responding the way Deoxys should respond. Rayquaza cannot identify what these things are. Its response is aggressive and causes collateral damage.

## 4. The Ancient Civilization

The people who were here before the researchers. They found the power spot, recognized it as something profound, and built their world carefully around it rather than through it.

Their legacy survives in:
- **Diamond carvings** — found in both Mirage Forest and at Dragon Frontiers, separated by ocean. These are records of what they witnessed. They document both Deoxys's arrival and Mew's response to it. They are background lore and world texture — they document history but do not deliver plot.
- **Stone domes at Holon Lake** — geometric carvings, great age, trees growing up through the openings. Still structurally sound.
- **The Holon Legacy at Dragon Frontiers** — a stone circle; the Japanese TCG name translates directly as "Holon's Holy Site." Marks a location of significance across an ocean from where Holon was eventually built.
- **Holon Ruins** — an abandoned building in Holon's architectural style with no canonical explanation for what it was. The researchers know about it but haven't been able to date it or explain why it looks the way it does. Steven will eventually identify what it actually is.
- **The Regi chambers** — three sealed chambers somewhere in the Mirage Forest ruins, housing Regirock, Regice, and Registeel. The ancient civilization sealed them here deliberately. The same people who built around the power spot rather than through it understood that some things should be left undisturbed.

The ancient civilization knew something was at the power spot. They didn't try to extract it, replicate it, or use it. Their sites are still standing.

## 5. Mew

Thousands of years ago — long before any researcher arrived — Mew came to the crash site.

As the genetic ancestor of all Pokémon life on Earth, Mew was drawn to Deoxys: a form of life with no connection whatsoever to the tree Mew anchors. Something completely outside its experience. The ancient civilization witnessed this. They documented it. The diamond carvings show both Mew and Deoxys at the same location.

The researchers spent their entire careers searching for Mew in Mirage Forest because of these legends. They built the Tower specifically to find it. They were standing on the answer the whole time: Mew was here, thousands of years ago, drawn to the thing buried beneath their feet.

Mew has returned. Drawn back by the same pull — Deoxys still buried, still radiating, the Tower's years of broadcast amplifying that signal into something Mew could feel from a distance. It had been circling the power spot for weeks before the accident, getting closer, undetected. Its proximity was the variable nobody accounted for.

**Mew ☆ δ** is what Mew became at the moment of the fracture — caught at the epicenter, transformed permanently by the surge. A Water-type Delta. It did not choose this. It was simply unable to stay away from the one thing on Earth it had never been able to understand, and it got too close at the wrong moment.

Mew is not catchable during the main story. After the fracture it moves ahead of the spreading phenomenon, tracking the fragments across the region — unseen, uncatchable, carrying the consequence of what its proximity caused. Its role in the ending and its availability in the postgame are covered in those sections.

## 6. The Accident — Full Sequence

### 6.1 Before

The Holon Research Tower has been broadcasting continuously since construction. Over years of operation, its electromagnetic field has slowly transformed the local Pokémon into Delta Species — a side effect the research team has been studying as a discovery without understanding they are the cause. The Delta mutations around Holon are the Tower's fingerprint: dual Metal typing at final evolution, base type changes restricted to Fire and Lightning, the unusual Holon's Pokémon that can transform into Energy.

Unknown to anyone, Mew has returned to the region. Drawn back by the same pull that brought it to the crash site thousands of years ago — the presence of Deoxys, the one form of life on Earth completely outside its experience — Mew has been circling the power spot for weeks, getting closer. Its proximity goes undetected. The Tower's instruments are not calibrated for what Mew's genetic field looks like in the data. What appears in the logs as minor anomalous readings is dismissed as equipment noise.

The faction has been pushing for increased broadcast power. They believe a stronger signal will let them track Delta mutations further out across the region. The lead researcher is reluctant. The faction's case is scientific and reasonable. The increase is approved.

### 6.2 The Surge

With the Tower broadcasting at increased power and Mew closer to the buried stone than it has been in thousands of years, the combined effect crosses a threshold nobody knew existed.

The Tower was built to find Mew — its broadcast frequency calibrated specifically to detect the EM signatures associated with Mew sightings in Mirage Forest. When Mew finally gets close enough, the Tower does exactly what it was designed to do: it detects Mew, and for the first time in its operational history, it begins receiving a return signal. The broadcast-and-receive loop it was built for completes.

The problem is that the Tower has been broadcasting into the buried stone for years. The completed signal — the Tower's frequency now carrying Mew's proximity as a reflected return — reaches the stone as something neither force could have produced alone. Deoxys's dormant biology doesn't read it as the Tower's frequency. It reads it as contact from something alive.

Deoxys responds. The dormant stone tablet activates.

The activation is not clean. Deoxys's forme-cycling mechanism — the real-time DNA rewriting that shifts it between formes — triggers involuntarily under the unnatural stimulus. The Delta phenomenon, which has been radiating passively from the buried stone for thousands of years and actively from the Tower into the surrounding ecosystem, surges outward as the stone cracks. Each forme that cycles through the activation absorbs a wave of that energy as it fragments.

Mew, at the epicenter of an EM surge it did not anticipate, is caught in the fracture event. The transformation is immediate and permanent. Mew becomes Mew ☆ δ — Water-type, marked by what it was present for, carrying the consequence of having gotten too close to something it could not stay away from.

### 6.3 The Fracture

The stone tablet cracks. Four Delta formes escape.

The **original stone** — the meteorite and the physical vessel — remains buried beneath the Tower. Cracked, dormant again, but present.

The four fragments scatter. Each one is a complete organism, biologically functional, but locked into a single behavioral optimization with no ability to shift. None of them is Deoxys. Each of them is part of what Deoxys was.

### 6.4 The Outward Pulse

The accident did not only wake what was buried beneath the Tower. The surge propagated outward into space as well as across the region. The Tower's instruments, overwhelmed by the local event, did not record this. The Volcano Observatory on the Unnamed Island did.

The outward pulse traveled at the speed of light. Anything in space sensitive to the Tower's frequency would receive it. For most things, it passed without effect. For Jirachi — dormant in deep space, its awakening cycle already stirring — the pulse hit at the wrong moment. It mimicked the comet's trigger frequency closely enough to begin rousing Jirachi from dormancy, and the Delta radiation saturating the pulse reached it in transit and transformed it before it fully oriented. Jirachi completed the journey to Holon already changed, waking into a nature it did not choose.

The observatory instruments tracked the outward pulse, then began detecting a faint return signal in the weeks that followed — growing stronger. The researchers' best reading is a comet on approach. They are correct. The EM signature it carries is a reflection of the pulse that woke it.

This detail is unknown to the research team in the immediate aftermath of the accident. It becomes known through the observatory's data across the main game's three visits, and resolves in the postgame when the comet arrives.

### 6.5 After

The Delta phenomenon, already present around Holon from years of Tower broadcast, surges outward beyond anything the research team can contain. The four fragments move through the region, each radiating its own version of the phenomenon as it goes — carrying it to places the Tower's signal never reached.

Rayquaza descends.

The faction, having advocated for the increased broadcast that preceded the accident, believes the surge was caused by pushing the Tower too hard. They feel responsible. Their pivot to exploitation is partly motivated by needing to believe the cost was worth something. They are not entirely wrong about their role — the increased broadcast was a contributing factor, closing the last gap between almost enough and enough. But it was not the cause. The cause was the Tower completing its purpose for the first time at the worst possible moment, and neither the faction nor the research team has yet read the Tower's pre-accident logs carefully enough to see the second signal in the data — the stone pulsing back a fraction of a second before everything overloaded. It reads as instrument noise. It isn't.

That detail is still there. Waiting.

## 7. The Central Conflict

### 7.1 The Two Causes

The Delta phenomenon has two distinct but connected origins, and understanding the difference between them is central to understanding the crisis.

**The Tower's ongoing broadcast** is responsible for the Delta mutations around Holon specifically. Years of continuous electromagnetic saturation slowly rewired the biology of local Pokémon. This produced the Metal secondary typing, the restricted type changes to Fire and Lightning, and the Holon's Pokémon that can transform into Energy. This version of Delta has been present for years and, around Holon itself, was relatively stable.

**The surge and Deoxys's fracture** is responsible for the spread. Without the accident, Delta would have remained a localised Holon phenomenon. The fracture sent four radiating fragments across the region, carrying the phenomenon to locations the Tower's signal never reached. The rules are different at those locations because the cause is different — fragment energy rather than Tower broadcast, operating without the Tower's Metal frequency as a carrier.

The faction conflates these two causes, treating the stable Tower-caused Delta around Holon as evidence that the spreading fragment-caused Delta will also stabilise. This is a fundamental error in their reasoning, even when their data is accurate.

### 7.2 The Ecological Crisis

The spread is accelerating. In the weeks and months since the accident, the electromagnetic field radiating from the fragments has been transforming the ecosystems of every location it touches. Pokémon are changing type mid-life, disrupting food chains and predator-prey relationships that evolved over thousands of years. Migration patterns are breaking down because the environmental signals Pokémon navigate by — temperature, magnetism, scent — are being overwritten by the EM field.

The fragments are not stable. Each one is locked in a single behavioral extreme with no ability to moderate itself, and that instability is what drives the accelerating spread. The Defense fragment doesn't move but its field grows because it has no Attack or Speed to balance it. The Attack fragment causes damage because it has no Defense or Normal to temper it. Left alone, the fragments don't equilibrate — they intensify. The ecosystem disruption isn't a temporary side effect. It's what happens when Deoxys exists in a broken state indefinitely.

Crucially, each fragment doesn't just spread Delta — it spreads its specific behavioral extreme into the ecosystems it touches. Pokémon near the Defense fragment become locked in rigid patterns: migration stops, food chains calcify, nothing adapts. Pokémon near the Attack fragment lose the context for their aggression — predator-prey dynamics collapse into directionless reactivity. The region isn't only changing types. It is being slowly reorganized around four pathological behavioral templates, each amplifying as the fragment that caused it continues to radiate unchecked.

### 7.3 The Faction

A splinter group within the research community has decided that the Delta phenomenon isn't a disaster to be reversed — it's an opportunity. The type mutations are real, stable, and reproducible. Pokémon transformed by Delta are in many cases stronger, rarer, more capable. If the phenomenon can be studied, mapped, and eventually controlled, it would be the most significant discovery in the history of Pokémon research.

Their grand goal is not passive. They want to deliberately re-fire the Tower at full capacity — not an accident this time, but an intentional, controlled broadcast across the entire region. Their belief is that Delta transformation is the next stage of Pokémon development, and what Holon already shows is proof it works. The accident was a tragedy of circumstance; the phenomenon itself is a gift. They want to finish what the accident started, cleanly, on their terms, for everyone.

This gives their escalation throughout the game a clear shape. Early on they are studying the fragments. Mid-game they are actively interfering with containment efforts, because the fragments radiating is their proof-of-concept data and containment ends the experiment. Late game it becomes clear they have been quietly modifying the Tower itself — preparing it for a second deliberate broadcast. The confrontation on the floating island is a race to stop them from firing before they understand what firing into four already-amplifying fragments would actually do. Their fundamental error — treating fragment-caused Delta as equivalent to Tower-caused Delta, assuming it will stabilise — is not an intellectual mistake at this point. It is a motivated one. They need it to be stable. Their entire position depends on it.

This group isn't evil in a simple sense. They are scientists who made a calculated decision to prioritise what can be gained over what is being lost, and have since stopped being able to revise that decision. Their argument is not entirely wrong. That's what makes it a real conflict.

Like the player, the faction is also gradually uncovering the truth of what caused the accident. They are pursuing this investigation for their own reasons — they need to understand the cause in order to control the phenomenon — and their findings converge toward the same truth the player is approaching from a different direction. They reach it at roughly the same time, and draw opposite conclusions from it.

The tension between the main research team — trying to understand and contain the accident — and the faction — trying to harness it — is the human story running underneath the ecological one. Both sides will seek the player's help at different points in the game.

### 7.4 The Player's Role

The player arrives in Holon as a new research assistant — young, not yet affiliated with either faction, brought in because the main team needs more hands after the accident overwhelmed their capacity. They didn't cause the accident, but they inherit its consequences.

The mystery of what truly caused the fracture unfolds naturally through the world, its Pokémon, its people, and the occasional appearance of Steven Stone at significant moments. Nothing is handed to the player directly. The truth accumulates. By the time it is stated plainly, the player is already most of the way there.

## 8. The Four Delta Deoxys Fragments

The Delta spread follows the paths of the four fragments. The further a fragment travels, the further the phenomenon reaches. The player encounters each fragment over the course of the game, building toward an understanding of what needs to happen.

### 8.1 Normal δ — Colorless — Mirage Forest / Holon outskirts

Deoxys's baseline. The most communicative forme, the most oriented toward contact. In fracture, this reads as disorientation — searching for the other formes, unable to find them, unable to understand what it is now. Wandering near Holon. Clearly anomalous, not threatening. The first fragment the player encounters, before they fully understand what they're looking at. This encounter establishes the emotional register: something is wrong with this creature, and it isn't dangerous so much as lost.

### 8.2 Defense δ — Metal — Holon Lake

Deoxys's survival instinct. In fracture, this manifests as total stasis — the fragment has barely moved from the lake area. Closed off. Static. Anchored by an imperative to endure that has no outlet and no counterbalance from the other formes. The hardest one to approach. The lake structure — three pillars around a glowing sphere — activates in its presence. The faction has been observing it from a distance and believes it's stable enough to study indefinitely. They are wrong about what stable means here.

### 8.3 Attack δ — Darkness — Unnamed Island

Deoxys's threat response. In fracture, this manifests as aggression without context — reactive, dangerous, fixated on perceived threats. This is the fragment causing most of the collateral damage that Rayquaza is responding to. The Unnamed Island's crystal formations are being affected by its presence in ways the faction finds extremely interesting. The player reaches this fragment at a point in the story where the faction's agenda has become fully clear, making this encounter the most directly contested.

### 8.4 Speed δ — Lightning — Dragon Frontiers

Deoxys's flight response. In fracture, this manifests as pure erratic movement — the fragment has traveled furthest from origin. It is in the Dragon Frontiers, somewhere on the lower island, never staying in one place. The one that ran. The last one found. Speed δ has been at Dragon Frontiers long enough that the Delta phenomenon there has stabilised into something organic — 100% Delta at final evolution, no Metal secondary. What the Tower forced through broadcast, Speed δ's sustained presence made natural through time.

## 9. Delta Species — Rules and Distribution

Delta Species is the phenomenon of Pokémon presenting different elemental types than their species normally would. It is not random — it follows geographic and proximity-based rules tied to two causes: the Tower's ongoing broadcast and the presence of the four fragments.

| Location | Cause | Delta Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Holon | Tower broadcast (years of saturation) | Dual Metal secondary at final evolution; base type changes to Fire, Lightning, or pure Metal |
| Holon Lake | Fragment — Defense δ | Nearly all Pokémon are Delta; anomalous patterns (Meowth δ is dual Metal unevolved; Magikarp δ starts Metal) |
| Unnamed Island | Fragment — Attack δ | Least prevalent Delta; changes only at final evolution; last location with dual Metal |
| Dragon Frontiers | Fragment — Speed δ (longest exposure) | 100% Delta at final evolution; NO dual Metal — natural Delta |

### 9.1 Why Dragon Frontiers Is Different

Distance from the Tower doesn't determine Delta intensity — time of exposure and the nature of the cause does. The Speed δ fragment left first, traveled furthest, and has been at Dragon Frontiers the longest. The phenomenon there has had the most time to settle into the ecosystem. It's not weaker for being far — it's more complete, more embedded, more natural.

Metal is the Tower's fingerprint, not Delta's. Pokémon close to Holon were mutated by the Tower's specific EM frequency directly, and Metal is baked into those mutations because Metal is what the Tower resonates at. Dragon Frontiers Pokémon were never exposed to the Tower's signal — only to Speed δ in isolation, which radiates pure Delta energy without the Tower's Metal overtone. Their Delta is cleaner. More purely the fragment's own influence, undiluted by the Tower's machinery.

This distinction matters narratively. The researchers assume Dragon Frontiers Delta must be weaker because it's far away. When the player arrives and finds 100% fully-evolved Delta with no Metal secondary, it reframes everything they thought they understood about the phenomenon's relationship to the Tower.

### 9.2 The Pulse Beyond Holon

The accident's EM surge did not stop at the region's edge. The fracture event sent a burst of radiation outward through the ground, the sea, and the upper atmosphere — propagating far beyond anything the Tower's sustained broadcast had ever reached. For most Pokémon at that distance, the pulse passed without lasting effect. But for some, it left a frequency they could not stop perceiving.
Legendaries drawn toward Holon approach through the filter of their own nature: Zapdos registering the EM band as something kin to its own electricity; Raikou tracking it the way it would a storm; Lugia receiving it as deep-ocean interference propagating up through the water. They are not transformed when they arrive. The Tower's broadcast does to them what it did to Holon's ecosystem — slowly, through sustained proximity. The same process that rewired local Pokémon over years works faster on biology operating at their scale, but it is still the same process. They came here on their own. The region changed them by being what it is.
The player is never hunting them across the wider world. The world is bringing them here.

### 9.3 The Three-Node Pattern

In the TCG, each set has a Stadium card named "Holon [X]" showing a structure with 3 nodes around a central point, resembling the Metal Energy symbol from above. These structures appear at every location with Delta Species presence:

| Location | Structure |
|---|---|
| Holon village | The Research Tower and its three satellite domed structures |
| Holon Lake | Three stone pillars around a central glowing sphere, rising from the water |
| Unnamed Island | Holon Circle — three tall crystals in a circular crater |
| Dragon Frontiers | Holon Legacy — three tall standing stones around a central stone |

Whether these structures are connected — multiple ancient power spots, markers left by the civilization, something the Delta phenomenon itself responds to — is not resolved in the source material and is deliberately left open in the game.

### 9.4 Ampharos δ — Story Note

Ampharos δ has a mechanic in the TCG (Holon Veil) that causes all Pokémon on its owner's field to be treated as Delta Species. This detail is reserved as a story beat or mission within the game rather than a gameplay mechanic. A wild or researcher-owned Ampharos δ whose field effect is somehow active could be relevant to understanding how the Delta phenomenon propagates — or could be a clue about the relationship between the Tower's broadcast and the fragment-caused spread.

## 10. Legendaries & Mythicals

The following legendaries and mythicals appear across the five TCG sets. Their placement is sourced directly from the cards. Roles are indicated as main story, optional, or postgame.

### 10.1 Regirock, Regice, Registeel — Mirage Forest

The three Gold Stars in EX Legend Maker, set in Mirage Forest before Delta Species exists in the story. The Regis live in sealed chambers within the Mirage Forest ruins — placed there deliberately by the ancient civilization, who understood what it meant to build around something rather than through it. They predate the accident entirely and are not Delta.

Steven Stone identifies the Regi chambers before the player does. He has been piecing together the ancient civilization's architecture throughout the game, and the Regi seals are the clearest expression of their philosophy: some things should be left undisturbed. He won't open the chambers himself. He tells the player they exist and leaves the decision to them.

**Role:** Optional late-game encounters, accessible after the main story concludes. Three separate chambers in the Mirage Forest ruins area.

### 10.2 Latias δ and Latios δ — Holon Village and Holon Lake

The Eon Pokémon appear across EX Delta Species and EX Holon Phantoms as Lightning/Metal dual-types — Tower-caused Delta, carrying the Metal fingerprint of the broadcast that transformed them. In the game they are a roaming pair: still moving, still bonded to each other, transformed but not separated. They thread through the first two major locations.

The faction wants to capture a Latias δ or Latios δ as a proof of concept for controlled Delta transformation. This is a mission the player can intercept. The pair is never stationary; encountering either one is a memorable event rather than a fixed encounter point.

**Role:** Roaming legendaries across Holon and Holon Lake. Story-adjacent through the faction's pursuit of them.

### 10.3 Rayquaza δ — Dragon Frontiers

Rayquaza δ appears in EX Delta Species, but in the game it belongs at Dragon Frontiers as the territorial encounter blocking access to the floating island. Its card placement in the early set reflects the researchers documenting its existence — Rayquaza has always been in the upper atmosphere above this region. It descends in response to the fragments doing things Deoxys has never done in its territory.

The Rayquaza encounter at Dragon Frontiers is not a straightforward battle. Rayquaza is not an obstacle to defeat so much as a territorial creature that has to be met on its own terms. Resolving the encounter clears the path to the floating island.

**Role:** Required story encounter at Dragon Frontiers.

### 10.4 Mewtwo δ — Holon Lake

Mewtwo δ is Fire/Metal in EX Delta Species — Tower-caused Delta — but the Gold Star Mewtwo ☆ appears at Holon Lake. Mewtwo is a created organism: artificial, psychic, with no natural territory. The EM field saturating Holon Lake affects Mewtwo in ways specific to what it is. It is here for reasons connected to its own nature that the researchers don't fully understand and that Steven has theories about but keeps to himself.

**Role:** Significant optional encounter at Holon Lake.

### 10.5 Groudon ☆ and Kyogre ☆ — Holon area

Both Gold Stars appear non-Delta in EX Delta Species — present at the location of the accident but apparently unaffected. They are too powerful or too fundamental for the Tower's field to rewrite. They have been drawn toward the EM disruption the way powerful Pokémon are drawn to unusual energy sources, circling and present but not transformed.

The fact that they didn't become Delta is itself a data point: some Pokémon resist transformation, and understanding why matters for understanding the phenomenon's limits.

**Role:** Optional deep postgame encounters.

### 10.6 Celebi ☆ — Unnamed Island

Celebi's Gold Star appearance on the Unnamed Island is quietly strange — an island with no ruins, no recorded history, no explanation for the crystals, and Celebi somewhere in the jungle. Celebi may have been coming to this island for reasons that predate the Delta phenomenon entirely, drawn by something the crystals do that has nothing to do with Deoxys or the Tower.

**Role:** Optional postgame encounter. The connection between Celebi and the island's crystals is left deliberately unexplained.

### 10.7 Alakazam ☆ — Unnamed Island

Alakazam's Gold Star on the Unnamed Island alongside Celebi and the power-granting crystals suggests the island does something specific to psychic and cognitive potential. No further explanation is given in the source material.

**Role:** Optional postgame encounter.

### 10.8 Charizard ☆ δ — Dragon Frontiers

A Darkness-type Delta — fully natural, no Metal secondary, completely transformed by Speed δ's sustained presence rather than the Tower's broadcast. In the game it is a rare wild encounter on the lower island: the visual proof that the Delta phenomenon at Dragon Frontiers is something categorically different. It isn't explained. The player finds one and understands.

**Role:** Rare wild encounter at Dragon Frontiers.

### 10.9 Mew ☆ δ — Floating Island / Mirage Forest

Covered in the Ending Sequence and Postgame sections below.

### 10.10 Jirachi δ — Unnamed Island Volcano (Postgame)

Not a TCG canon Delta form — invented for the postgame. Jirachi's dormancy cycle was already stirring when the outward pulse reached it in deep space. The pulse mimicked the comet's trigger frequency closely enough to begin the awakening, but the Delta radiation saturating it reached Jirachi before it had fully oriented — transforming it in transit, mid-cycle, before it understood where it was or what was happening. It arrived in Holon already changed, having woken wrong.

Jirachi δ is not a corrupted or broken Jirachi. It is a fundamental transformation of its nature — wish-granting replaced entirely by disaster and doom. A comet has always carried two faces: shooting star for a wish, vehicle for destruction. The Delta radiation woke the doom side. Its presence on the Unnamed Island has been causing volcanic eruptions, destabilising the island's crystals, and driving Pokémon from the volcano area. It is dormant inside the caldera, drawn to the Holon Circle crystals as the closest frequency in the region to what its comet should have felt like.

The observatory throughline pays off here. When the postgame opens, the castaway has new data: the comet arrived, and it went to the volcano. The Jirachi δ quest begins from this moment.

**Type:** Dark / Fire

**Visual:** Charcoal and ember-red replacing gold and white. Star points jagged like cracked volcanic rock with heat visible underneath. The three body dots glowing molten orange. The wish tags burnt black and curled at the edges. The crystals in the caldera chamber around it have gone dark red from the heat.

**Mission:** Jirachi Doombringer — a classic legendary dungeon mission. The volcano's eruption activity has opened new passages into the caldera. The dungeon is the volcano interior. The final chamber is the Holon Circle at the caldera base.

**Quest giver:** The castaway.

**Role:** Major postgame mission. Primary encounter of the space-origin postgame arc.

### 10.11 Rayquaza δ, Latias δ + Latios δ, Lugia δ — Postgame (Space-Origin Arc)

Four additional Delta legendaries drawn to the region by the outward pulse, transformed by the Delta radiation on approach. These are separate postgame encounters, each at a different location. Their specific placement and encounter structures are to be determined. All are connected to the observatory throughline — the castaway's instruments tracked their approach before and after the main story's conclusion. TCG canon Delta forms are used where they exist (Rayquaza δ: Fire/Metal, Latias δ and Latios δ: Lightning or Fire/Metal). Lugia δ is an invented form.

## 11. Locations & World Geography

All named locations and sub-areas below are sourced directly from card art across the five TCG sets, plus connective areas needed to make the region function as a game world. Canonical TCG sources are noted. Invented connective areas are marked accordingly.

---

### 11.1 Progression Structure

The game follows a linear spine with Holon village as a recurring narrative anchor. The player always moves forward through the five major locations in order, but returns to Holon at key story beats. Progression is gated by HMs in the traditional Gen 3 style. Gyms are present and function identically to standard Pokémon games. In-universe they are lightly rebranded as research stations or field outposts whose station chiefs are senior researchers or expedition leads — the structural equivalent of Gym Leaders. Whether the word "Gym" is used in-universe is a low-priority decision that doesn't affect structure.

---

### 11.2 Major Locations

#### 11.2.1 Mirage Forest

*TCG source: EX Legend Maker*

The entrance to the region and the player's starting area. A dense forest with flora reminiscent of the Jurassic period — ancient-looking tree ferns, exposed rock, clearings. Home to Fossil Pokémon and the legends of Mew. The power spot is buried here. Holon village is built at its center.

Sub-areas sourced from card art:

- **Sand Basin** — a sunken desert clearing within the forest interior. Sourced from the Trapinch card. Unusual within the surrounding forest; feels like a wrong turn into somewhere older.
- **Ice Plateau** — a frozen highland area associated with the Spheal and Sealeo line. A cold-air basin at higher elevation at the forest's edge.
- **The Cursed Shrine** — a cave shrine entrance with a history of local superstition. The ancient civilization built it. The researchers have catalogued it but not excavated it.
- **Full Flame Clearing** — a Poké Ball-shaped ring of inextinguishable flames in the center of a clearing. The Japanese card name (*Lasting Flame*) suggests the fire has never gone out. No explanation is given.
- **The Power Tree Clearing** — a giant tree at the center of a clearing, connected in name to the power spot. The Giant Stump nearby raises the question of what it replaced.
- **Strange Cave** — a cave filled with stalactites and stalagmites, lit by soft yellow bioluminescent light. Source of the light is not identified.
- **The Railway** — a short section of disused rail track visible in the Japanese Voltorb card. No station, no origin or destination. Just track emerging from and disappearing back into the undergrowth. Who laid it and why is unknown.

**Entrance Town** *(invented connector, sourced from Mirage's Mew starter deck)*: A modern town at the forest's edge — stone brick buildings, paved streets, glass windows, metal streetlights, electrical lighting. The last ordinary settlement before the forest starts. Distinct from Holon village; predates or exists independently of the research team. This is where the player begins the game.

---

#### 11.2.2 Holon Village & the Research Tower

*TCG source: EX Delta Species*

The research village at the heart of Mirage Forest. From above, the layout resembles a giant Poké Ball: a ring of trees as the outer border, a canal running through the diameter, two circular waterways surrounding the village center. Nine buildings with round or rectangular forms and sloped red roofs, many with farms or orchards attached. The centermost building is the largest, with an observatory dome. Wild Pokémon inhabit the canals and the surrounding forest.

The **Holon Research Tower** stands at the absolute center: a four-storey triangular tower with a large upward-facing metal dish above the domed roof. Three smaller domed structures surround it in a triangle formation, with lightning arcing between them and the Tower when active. Viewed from above, the Tower and its satellite structures form the symbol used on Metal Energy cards. The Tower has been broadcasting continuously since construction — the ongoing source of Holon's localised Delta mutations.

The **Tower interior** is split between two distinct architectural modes: a normal stone interior in some areas, and an oddly organic interior in others — uneven walls and pillars, a large amount of glowing circular machinery. The organic section may be confined to the centermost building. Whether this appearance predates the research team or developed through proximity to what is buried beneath the Tower is not resolved.

The **Holon Ruins** stand elsewhere in the village: an abandoned building in the Holon architectural style with no canonical explanation. The researchers know about it and have not been able to date it. It predates the current research team. Steven will eventually identify what it actually is.

A **river runs past the village** exterior, feeding the internal canal system. This river is a route connector, running upstream toward the forest or downstream toward the coast.

The village experiences **weather variation** including snow. The Day/Night System applies here.

---

#### 11.2.3 Holon Lake

*TCG source: EX Holon Phantoms*

South of Holon village, described in the set blurb as "the unknown lands beyond the labs of Holon." A large lake surrounded by deciduous forest with an intersecting mountain range of jagged blue rock. The far end of the range breaks down into scattered stone spikes. Nearly all Pokémon encountered here are Delta Species — caused by Defense δ's presence rather than the Tower's broadcast, which is why the rules are different here.

Sub-areas sourced from card art:

- **Stone Spike Badlands** — where the mountain range fades into isolated stone columns rising from the ground. Sourced from the Anorith card. Geological rather than forested; visually unlike anything else in the region.
- **Fern Lowlands** — dense fern undergrowth on the approaches to the lake. Sourced from the POP Series 5 Meowth card. A third distinct ground-level environment type separate from the long grasses and marshland.
- **The Stone Dome Grove** — a cluster of large ancient stone domes covered in geometric carvings. Each dome contains a tree, with branches growing through circular openings in the stone. Still structurally intact. The Holon Adventurer card shows an explorer discovering these structures — they are not common knowledge even among the research team. One of the clearest surviving pieces of ancient civilization infrastructure.
- **The Lake Structure** — a structure rising from the water composed of three stone pillars in a triangle formation around a central stone sphere. When active, the central sphere glows bright blue. Depicted in multiple cards in both active and inactive states. Defense δ's presence in this area correlates with its activity state. What it was built for is not explained.

**Marshland Settlement** *(invented connector)*: A small research outpost on the marshland side of the lake. The only human presence in this area beyond the village. Functions as a mid-area rest point and the base from which researchers have been observing the stone domes and the lake structure.

---

#### 11.2.4 Unnamed Island

*TCG source: EX Crystal Guardians*

A remote tropical volcanic island — the largest in an archipelago — reached by boat. Rocky coastlines and palm-lined beaches, with rocky arches and isolated sea stacks offshore. The surrounding ocean has mangroves near shore and a coral reef in deeper water. Island interior is jungle-like with Jurassic-similar flora mixed with tall thin conifers. Multiple smoking volcanoes. The island suffers from windstorms. Delta Species are least prevalent here — caused by Attack δ's presence rather than Tower broadcast, and Attack δ arrived more recently than the other fragments.

The most immediately striking feature: **glowing crystals** cover the entire island. Pink and pale blue are most abundant. They appear in rocky areas, cave systems, and throughout the underground. The crystals are described in the TCG as granting special powers if used correctly.

Sub-areas sourced from card art:

- **Crystal Caves** — an underground cave system with crystals embedded throughout the rock. Sourced from the Sableye card. The surface crystal deposits are the visible top of a much larger subterranean system.
- **Holon Circle** — a circular crater containing three tall pink-purple crystals in a triangle formation. The only named location on this island, following the three-node pattern present at every Delta location.
- **The Satellite Islets** — smaller islands visible in the surrounding ocean, sourced from the Numel card. Not full locations but navigable: a rocky islet, a reef island, a small cave islet.
- **Algae Rocks** — a section of coastline with algae-covered rocks, sourced from the Ludicolo card.

The island has **no known permanent human settlement** beyond the observatory. The absence of ancient ruins here — unlike every other location in the region — is itself a detail. The crystals were described as usable, implying prior knowledge of how to use them, but whoever had that knowledge left no structures behind. The Holon Circle is the only exception: three crystals arranged deliberately in a crater, clearly intentional.

**The Volcano Observatory** *(invented)*: Built by the Holon research team on the rim of the island's largest active volcano — the same team that built the Tower. The Tower was looking inward at the power spot; the observatory was built to look outward at the sky and space, away from the Tower's own EM interference. The island's volcanic rock has an unusually low magnetic signature, and the crystals act as a natural amplifier for incoming EM frequencies, making this the most sensitive observation site in the region. A domed copper-green roof, partially carved into the volcanic rock, with satellite dishes and banks of recording equipment inside. The observatory was operational during the accident and recorded the outward pulse in full — data the Tower's own instruments could not capture because the Tower was inside the event. The observatory is locked on the player's first visit. It unlocks mid-game when the lead researcher finds the castaway's buried access request and sends the player back with the code.

**The Castaway** *(TCG: Castaway trainer card)*: The observatory's last assigned researcher — not a stranded sailor but a scientist who chose to stay after the accident when his instruments showed something nobody at Holon came to collect. He sent his data summary the day after the accident; in the chaos of the ecological crisis it went unread. He has been on the island since, maintaining the instruments and filling logbooks. He is the player's primary point of contact on the island and serves as quest giver for the Jirachi Doombringer postgame mission.

**The observatory throughline across the main game:** First visit — locked, player meets the castaway outside, learns the accident sent a signal into space. The castaway's instruments recorded the outward pulse and he sent a data summary to Holon the next day; in the chaos of the ecological crisis it went unread. He chose to stay. Mid-game unlock — the lead researcher finds the buried access request; player returns with the code and accesses the instruments for the first time. The logbooks show a faint return signal detected in the weeks after the accident, growing steadily stronger. Second visit — the return signal has resolved into something identifiable: a comet on approach, carrying an EM signature that matches the outward pulse frequency. The researchers read it as a comet. They are correct. The postgame's arrival is not a surprise — the player has known something was coming since their first visit.

**The Boat Port** *(invented connector)*: A small dock on the island's most accessible coastline. A single moored vessel and a weather station.

---

#### 11.2.5 Dragon Frontiers

*TCG source: EX Dragon Frontiers*

Two islands "beyond the horizon": a lower rocky island and a floating island above it, encircled by a ring of clouds. Nearly all Pokémon here are Delta Species at full evolution, with no dual Metal secondary type — caused by Speed δ's presence over the longest period of any fragment, producing natural stabilised Delta rather than Tower-caused mutation.

Sub-areas sourced from card art:

- **Red Cliff Valley** — Sedona-like interior of the lower island. Staggered red sandstone cliffs, isolated pillar formations, sparse dry scrub. The most visually distinct environment in the region.
- **Dragon Frontiers Swamp** — inland wetland on the bank of water on the lower island. The most isolated environment in the game. Home to the most unusual natural Delta Pokémon.
- **The Sunken Stone** — a large block of clearly worked stone, cut at right angles, half-submerged in the sea off the coast. Artificial. Enormous. Connects to the ancient civilization but raises questions no other site raises — this one is in the ocean.
- **Holon Legacy** — a stone circle on the island's only beach: three tall standing stones in a triangle formation around a shorter central stone. The Japanese card name (*Holon's Holy Site*) describes it as a holy site of great age. The furthest point from Holon that still carries the Holon name.

The **floating island** sits above the lower island, ringed by cloud. The TCG cards never depict its surface — every card placing the viewer below the cloud ring. What is on top is entirely unknown within the source material. This is where the ending sequence begins. Its surface is for the game to define.

**The Hermit's Settlement** *(invented connector)*: A small shelter on the lower island's pine coast. Not a town — a fire, a structure, provisions. The hermit has been here long enough to have noticed things the researchers haven't.

## 12. Key Characters & Structure

### 12.1 Steven Stone

Steven arrives in Holon independently — drawn by reports of the Delta phenomenon and the unusual stones the accident produced — and has been here since shortly after the accident. He is not part of the research team but not an outsider either. He approaches the region as a geologist and collector: not treating the phenomenon as an EM problem to be solved but as a geological and biological transformation to be understood through physical evidence.

He appears at key moments throughout the game — never traveling with the player, always having arrived somewhere first or turning up unexpectedly at a significant location. Each appearance reframes something the player has already seen. He shares observations without stating conclusions. He is the one who identifies what the Holon Ruins actually are. He is the one who locates the Regi chambers and leaves the decision of opening them to the player. By the endgame he understands more about what caused the accident than anyone — not because he was told, but because he read the evidence that everyone else dismissed as noise.

Steven is the Champion. His team naturally includes Delta Pokémon encountered during his time in Holon — Steel types and Holon Metals. His final conversation with the player before they climb to the floating island is the last context they need. He has figured out what is up there and what Mew's role in all of it actually was. He says what he knows. Then he steps back, because it isn't his call.

After the ending he leaves Holon. He gives the player his documentation of what the Tower actually is and what the meteorite actually was — written in his careful hand. The researchers will study it for years. He goes to find the next thing.

### 12.2 Progression & Gyms

The game uses standard Gen 3 gym and HM structure. Eight gyms, a Pokémon League, Steven as Champion. Gyms are lightly rebranded in-universe as research stations or field outposts whose leaders are senior researchers or expedition leads. HMs function identically to Gen 3. Whether the word "Gym" appears in-universe is a low-priority flavour decision.

Each station chief is a researcher who has been living with the Delta phenomenon in their location. Their team reflects what the spread has done to that area. Defeating them demonstrates that the player understands the phenomenon well enough to operate in that environment.

## 13. The Ending Sequence

### 13.1 Act Structure

**Act 1 — Mirage Forest to Holon Village.** The player arrives, learns about the accident, encounters the first signs of ecological disruption. Meets the research team. Normal δ is the first fragment encounter — disoriented, not threatening. The faction is present but not yet clearly adversarial.

**Act 2 — Holon Lake and Unnamed Island.** The ecological damage becomes serious and visible. The faction's agenda becomes clear. Defense δ and Attack δ are encountered. The midpoint return to Holon is where the player learns the spread is accelerating and containment is failing. The faction argues this is proof that containment is the wrong goal.

**Act 3 — Dragon Frontiers and resolution.** The full picture comes together. Speed δ, Rayquaza, the floating island. Steven appears — his final scene before the climb, where he tells the player what the pre-accident logs actually show. Two signals. The player understands what happened. Then the climb.

### 13.2 On the Floating Island

The faction arrives at the same time as the player — they have reached the same conclusion through their own investigation and drawn the opposite conclusion from it. They have also finished preparing the Tower for a second deliberate broadcast. The final confrontation isn't only ideological. They are here to send a signal back down to Holon and fire.

They believe that knowing Mew caused the fracture makes the phenomenon more extraordinary, not less, and that a controlled second pulse — this time intentional — will finish the transformation cleanly. They have not accounted for what a deliberate broadcast into four already-amplifying, already-spreading fragments would actually produce. Nobody has. That unknown is the horror underneath the climax.

The **final faction battle** takes place here. Two groups who found the same truth and chose differently.

Then the **four fractured Deoxys converge** on the floating island, drawn to the highest point furthest from Tower interference.

**Mew ☆ δ appears.**

Mew has been moving through the region since the fracture, tracking the fragments, carrying the mark of what its proximity caused. It doesn't restore Deoxys here. What Mew does is show the fragments the way back — it perceives the location of the original stone, still buried beneath the Tower, still carrying the residual signal of what Deoxys was. Mew acts as the bridge. The fragments orient toward it, and then toward something beyond the horizon.

### 13.3 The Return to Holon

The player returns to Holon village. The Tower is still there, cracked and quiet. The researchers are there. Steven is there. Something is happening underground.

The player descends beneath the Tower — into the basement never fully excavated, where the organic architecture is strangest and the glowing machinery has no obvious explanation — and finds the original stone. The meteorite. Cracked but present. The four fragments have arrived ahead of the player, drawn by the signal Mew showed them. They are gathered around the stone.

The **four fractured Deoxys δ encounter** happens here, at the source, in the place where it all began. The player engages with each fragment as they attempt to reconverge with the original vessel.

The restoration completes. **The player has the opportunity to catch Deoxys** at the moment of reconvergence — whole, restored, carrying the marks of having been broken and made complete again.

The EM field saturating the region begins to quiet. The Delta Pokémon that have been transformed don't revert — they're already changed, and that change is permanent — but the spread stops accelerating. The ecosystem disruption stabilises. The power spot energy returns to its old character: warm, quiet, healing.

Deoxys returns to the stone. The stone seals. The Tower, still standing above it, is now just a building again.

### 13.4 Steven's Final Scene

He has been in the lab when the player comes back up. He has looked at the readings. He says: we built this entire tower trying to find something that was already here. We were standing on it. Then he says he is leaving. He gives the player his documentation. The researchers will study it for years. He goes.

The credits roll over the region settling — Delta Pokémon in the wild carrying their new types, the ecosystem finding a new equilibrium. The Delta phenomenon didn't end. It stabilised. The region is different from what it was before the accident. It's also, in its own way, okay.

---

## 14. Postgame

With Deoxys restored and the faction defeated, the player completes the Pokémon League and defeats Steven as Champion. The region then opens fully. The urgency is gone. The postgame has the texture of a late-summer tropical Pokémon adventure — the world is yours to explore at your own pace.

The observatory throughline — built across three visits during the main game — means the player already knows something is coming before the postgame begins. By the third observatory visit, the castaway's instruments had resolved the return signal as a comet on approach carrying the pulse frequency. When the postgame opens, he has the final data: it arrived, and it went to the volcano.

### 14.1 Available Postgame Content

- **The Floating Island** — now fully accessible from above for the first time. Its surface is endgame territory; what is up there is for the game to define.
- **The Regi Chambers** — three sealed chambers in the Mirage Forest ruins. Steven has left notes. The player opens them or doesn't.
- **Groudon ☆ and Kyogre ☆** — somewhere in the deep wilderness around Holon, non-Delta, drawn to the region by the EM disruption.
- **Mewtwo δ** — at Holon Lake, still present.
- **The Satellite Islets** — the smaller islands off the Unnamed Island have secrets that weren't accessible during the main story.
- **Faction researchers** — some remain in the region. Some are still convinced they were right. All of them changed by being present for the restoration.
- **Jirachi Doombringer** — the primary postgame mission. See Legendaries section.
- **Rayquaza δ, Latias δ + Latios δ, Lugia δ** — additional space-origin Delta legendary encounters spread across the region. Placement and encounter structures to be determined.

### 14.2 The Mew ☆ δ Mission

After the ending, Mew ☆ δ slips away. It has done what it came to do. It goes back to being what Mew always is: somewhere in the region, unseen, moving.

The postgame Mew mission involves following the trail it left — ecological traces of a Water-type Delta Mew moving through each of the five locations after the restoration. One stop per location, finding evidence of its passage quietly woven into the world. The trail requires attention to notice.

The final encounter is back in Mirage Forest, where the story began. Mew went home. The researchers still haven't found it. The player does, because they understand the region in a way no researcher does after everything they have been through.

## 15. Themes

These are the three core thematic currents running through the game — not a message the game is trying to deliver, but patterns that emerge from what happens and what the characters choose.

**Preservation of all life.** The central ethical tension of the game. The faction wants to preserve and exploit the Delta phenomenon indefinitely, treating any attempt to stop it as the destruction of something extraordinary. Rayquaza's territorial aggression causes collateral damage to the very ecosystem it patrols. The researchers built a tower without considering what broadcasting into a living ecosystem for years would do to it. The player's goal — restoring Deoxys — is itself an act of preservation. The ending refuses to clean the slate: Delta Pokémon don't revert, the region carries permanent marks, and preservation here means ensuring life continues rather than restoring what was. The ancient civilization understood this — they built around the power spot rather than through it.

**Adaptation — life finds a way.** Delta Species are this theme made literal and mechanical. Pokémon exposed to EM radiation didn't die — they changed typing, found new ecological roles, and kept living. The Dragon Frontiers are the clearest expression: the location furthest from Holon hosts entirely natural Delta populations with no Metal secondary typing, meaning these are stable species, not mutations in crisis. Life adapted, then flourished. Deoxys itself is adaptive at the biological level — it rewrites its own DNA in real time, cycling formes as circumstances demand. The fracture breaks this adaptability by locking each fragment into a single forme. Restoration is the return of adaptive capacity. The ending's new equilibrium is the region itself adapting — different from what it was, and going to be okay.

**Scientific curiosity vs. restraint and unintended consequences.** The researchers came to find Mew — a pure scientific pursuit — and accidentally woke something buried for thousands of years, saturated an ecosystem with EM radiation, and sent a pulse outward that is still arriving. The Tower was built directly above the thing they were looking for without anyone realizing it. The ancient civilization had less data and less technology, and left the power spot undisturbed for millennia. The contrast is not anti-science — it is about what curiosity without restraint costs. The faction sharpens this: they are not wrong that the Delta phenomenon is real and extraordinary, and the player defeats them not because knowledge is dangerous but because the cost of continuing is other lives. The false ending deepens it further — the lead researcher's line when the postgame opens is the theme stated plainly: they never calculated how far the signal traveled, and whatever else received it is still coming.
