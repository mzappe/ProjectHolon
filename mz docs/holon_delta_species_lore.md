## 1. What Is Holon? — Overview

Holon is a TCG-exclusive location within Mirage Forest. It is a known habitat of and the apparent origin of the unusual δ Delta Species Pokémon. The name Holon comes from "holon," a philosophical concept of something that is simultaneously a whole and a part.

> **Note on set order:** Though officially referring only to the research village, the term Holon is often used by fans to refer collectively to the locations featured in the TCG sets from EX Delta Species to EX Dragon Frontiers. The English release order does **not** match the Japanese story order. EX Delta Species was released in English before EX Legend Maker, despite EX Legend Maker taking place first in the story.

## 2. Set 1 (Story Order) — EX Legend Maker

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese Title** | まぼろしの森 — *Mirage Forest* |
| **English Release** | February 13, 2006 |
| **Japanese Release** | June 30, 2005 |

### English Blurb

*Every Legend Has a Beginning... What secrets does Mew hide that Pokémon trainers have been waiting to learn? In EX Legend Maker, you'll unravel the truth behind the most mysterious Pokémon of all! With powerful ancient Pokémon to find, amazing new Stadiums to master, and a mythic Pokémon that every trainer wants to catch, this is the stuff legends are made of!*

### Setting

The expansion was set in the Mirage Forest, which is home to many rare Pokémon, including the elusive Mew.

### What Mirage Forest Looks Like

Mirage Forest is generally depicted as a dense forest with flora reminiscent of the Jurassic period. It is home to Fossil Pokémon and the Mythical Pokémon Mew. The forest's ground is rock-studded in places, and features a number of clearings. It contains an area of dead, leafless trees, and hilly areas of bare rock. It is seemingly located next to the sea, with a beach and pink coral seen on multiple cards. The Sealeo line also features a snowy ice sheet in their cards, which may be located in or around Mirage Forest. Additionally, one card features a smoking volcano within the forest.

### Evidence of Prior Civilization

The Japanese equivalent set Mirage Forest showed little evidence of human habitation, with the only artificial constructs being a cave shrine, some diamond shaped stone carvings, and a short section of railway. The complementary Mirage's Mew Constructed Starter Deck added a number of cards that feature man-made structures, including a settlement.

### Relationship to Holon

In Japan, this set was released before the introduction of Delta Species, and as such features no Delta Pokémon. This can be interpreted as showing areas of the forest unaffected by the Holon Research Tower, or as depicting the forest prior to the construction of Holon.

### Named Landmarks

- **Power Spot** — Deep in the forest is a place known as the power spot which is filled with a mysterious power. It was said that when people traveled there, energy welled throughout their body and they were healed. Holon was constructed on top of the power spot in order to study its effects.
- **Cursed Stone** — Depicted as a cave entrance; the Japanese name *Cursed Shrine* indicates that this is the entrance to a shrine.
- **Full Flame** — A small Poké Ball shaped ring of flames in the center of a clearing. The Japanese name *Lasting Flame* suggests that these flames are inextinguishable.
- **Power Tree** — A giant tree in the center of a clearing. The English name suggests a connection to the power spot, but no such similarity appears in the original Japanese, which translates as *Strange Large Tree*.
- **Giant Stump** — The cut stump of an even larger tree.
- **Strange Cave** — A cave filled with stalactites and stalagmites, lit by a mysterious soft yellow light.

### Notable Cards

The set features Mew and Mew ex as headline cards, as well as Fossil Pokémon including Aerodactyl, Kabuto/Kabutops, and Anorith/Armaldo. The secret rare card is Pikachu δ — the only Delta Species card in the English set, added due to the scrambled release order.

### Trivia

The depiction of Faraway Island in the anime shares many similarities with Mirage Forest and surrounding areas, such as Jurassic-esque foliage, wild Fossil Pokémon, glowing crystals, and the presence of Mew.

## 3. Set 2 (Story Order) — EX Delta Species

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese Title** | ホロンの研究塔 — *Holon Research Tower* |
| **English Release** | October 31, 2005 |
| **Japanese Release** | October 28, 2005 |

### English Blurb

*Behold the Newest Species of Pokémon! From the research labs in the tower of Holon comes a revelation that will thrill anyone who trains and collects Pokémon: there's a brand-new species of Pokémon! In EX Delta Species, players will share in the discovery of these rare and unique Dual Energy Pokémon, as well as experiment with cool new Holon Energy that will make Pokémon Delta Species even more powerful!*

### Setting and Origin of Delta Species

EX Delta Species introduced δ Delta Species, a type of Pokémon, and Holon's Pokémon, which could also double as Special Energy cards. The expansion was set in the Holon Research Tower, home of scientists who conduct research on Mew and strange new Pokémon. These Pokémon have been altered by a wave of electromagnetic radiation produced by an experiment gone awry at Holon labs. The result was a new species of Pokémon called the Delta Species. These new Pokémon possessed different types than their usual species.

### The Full Holon History

Holon was constructed by researchers to study the power spot, a location in Mirage Forest filled with a mysterious power said to heal people. Over time the research village and power spot came to be called Holon. The research tower at the center of Holon generated a strong magnetic field, which brought out the dormant power of Pokémon causing the Pokémon living around Holon to change type, with fully evolved Pokémon sometimes also gaining a secondary Metal type. The researchers named these Pokémon δ Delta Species. While studying these Pokémon, the researchers discovered another type of Pokémon capable of transforming into Energy, which they named Holon's Pokémon. Through studying these Delta and Holon's Pokémon, they were able to create three varieties of a new energy called Holon or synthetic Energy: FF, GL, and WP.

### Why the Researchers Came to Mirage Forest

The Japanese source cited by Bulbapedia (pokemon-card.com, archived November 2005) establishes that the researchers came specifically to search for and study Mew. The Holon Research Tower was built as an electromagnetic tracking tool aimed at locating Mew. The Delta transformation of local Pokémon was an unintended side effect of this research — **not the original goal.**

### The Village Layout

From above, the village looks like a giant Poké Ball, with a ring of trees serving as the main border and a canal that runs through the diameter into two circles that surround the village center. A river can be seen running past the city, presumably acting as the source of the canal. A cobbled walkway circles the land in between the circular canals and border, bridging the waterways. There is also a larger walkway with grand balustrades that leads to the village center. The village consists of nine buildings, which are round or rectangular with sloped red roofs, and many have a farm or orchard attached. The centermost building is the largest and includes an observatory dome on its roof.

### The Research Tower

At the very center of the village is the Holon Research Tower, a triangular tower four stories tall with a domed roof holding a large metal dish facing upwards above it. Three small structures with small metal domes on their roofs surround the tower in a triangle formation. Lightning can be seen arcing between the tower's dish and these domes when active. When viewed from above, the tower and surrounding structures form the symbol used on Metal Energy cards. The set symbol for EX Delta Species is based on the Holon Research Tower.

### The Mysterious Interior

Interior architecture can be seen on multiple cards, sometimes depicted as a normal stone interior, but equally as often depicted as oddly organic looking, containing uneven walls and pillars, as well as a large amount of glowing circular machinery. Based on the surroundings visible in some cards, this odd interior may be located inside the centermost building.

### The Holon Ruins

Holon also contains an abandoned building in the Holon architectural style called the Holon Ruins. No explanation for the ruins is given in the lore.

### Delta Species Behavior at Holon

δ Delta Species Pokémon here will almost always become dual Metal-type at their final evolutionary stage and uniquely may never change their base type at all. Those that do change base type will always change to Fire or Lightning type, or rarely to pure Metal type.

### Holon's Pokémon — Why Certain Species Are Affected

Of the Holon's Pokémon shown, the Magnemite and Voltorb lines are described in Pokédex entries as being attracted to and absorbing magnetism and electricity. Castform is described as having its cellular structure influenced by changes in climate, resulting in it changing type. These traits may explain why these particular Pokémon are strongly affected by the magnetic field.

### Card Design

Delta Species Pokémon had "δ Delta Species" between the name and HP, had a double helix on the border of the artwork window, and the Evolution box featured a δ design.

### Notable Cards

This is the set that introduces all of the above. The Eevee-lutions (Flareon δ, Jolteon δ, Vaporeon δ, Espeon δ, Umbreon δ) are all dual Metal type. Rayquaza δ, Mewtwo δ, Latias δ, and Latios δ are featured. Holon's Electrode, Holon's Magneton, Holon's Magnemite, and Holon's Voltorb are the Holon's Pokémon. Trainer cards include Holon Researcher, Holon Scientist, Holon Farmer, Holon Lass, Holon Mentor, Holon Transceiver, Holon Ruins, and the Holon Research Tower Stadium card. The three Holon Energy variants (FF, GL, WP) are introduced.

## 4. Set 3 (Story Order) — EX Holon Phantoms

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese Title** | ホロンの幻影 — *Holon Phantom* |
| **English Release** | May 3, 2006 |
| **Japanese Release** | January 27, 2006 |

### English Blurb

*Where Shadows Fall, Phantoms Dwell. What mysteries await the bold Trainer who ventures into the unknown lands beyond the labs of Holon? In EX Holon Phantoms, there's only one way to find out — step into the shadows! With more mysterious Pokémon Delta Species to discover, EX Holon Phantoms dares you to find what you've never seen before.*

### Setting

The expansion was set in Holon, south of the city around which EX Delta Species was based. Because of the more natural habitat, fewer of the Delta Species Pokémon are dual-type.

### What Holon Lake Looks Like

Holon Lake is described as "the unknown lands beyond the labs of Holon." It is depicted as a large lake surrounded by forest with an intersecting mountain range of jagged blue rock. The furthest end of the mountain range fades into a number of scattered stone spikes, while closer to the lake is a marshland and an area of long grasses. As with Holon, the surrounding forest is typically depicted as a deciduous forest, though some cards include flora similar to those found in the Mirage Forest set. Nearly all Pokémon found here are Delta Species or Holon's Pokémon.

### Mysterious Structures

- **Stone domes** — A number of cards depict odd stone domes covered in geometric carvings. The domes each contain a tree and have a number of circular openings which the branches grow through. The card Holon Adventurer shows an explorer amongst the structures.
- **Holon Lake structure** — A structure poking out of the lake composed of three stone pillars in a triangle formation around a central stone sphere. While active, the central sphere glows bright blue.

### Anomalies in Delta Behavior at Holon Lake

Holon Lake contains a number of Pokémon that do not follow previously established patterns:

- **Meowth δ** — Is a dual Metal-type despite not being fully evolved.
- **Magikarp δ / Gyarados δ** — Starts as Metal-type and evolves into a Lightning/Metal dual-type, rather than the expected progression of Lightning gaining a Metal dual-type.
- **Anorith δ / Armaldo δ** — Starts as Metal-type and evolves into a Fighting/Metal dual-type similarly to Magikarp, though Armaldo's usual type is Fighting, causing it to return to its typical type.

### Notable Cards

This set features all four formes of **Deoxys δ** as separate Rare Holo cards — Darkness, Metal, Colorless, and Lightning types in the English set, corresponding to the Attack, Defense, Normal, and Speed formes. This is the **only set in the entire Delta Series arc to feature Deoxys as a Delta Species.** Also included are Rayquaza δ (Fire, single type — no Metal secondary), Mewtwo δ (Lightning, single type), and Gyarados ☆ δ (Fire) as the Gold Star. Holon's Castform is introduced here. The Holon Lake Stadium card, Holon Adventurer, and δ Rainbow Energy are also introduced.

### Trivia

Holon Lake is also home to a Shining Gyarados, similar to the Red Gyarados that appeared at the Lake of Rage.

## 5. Set 4 (Story Order) — EX Crystal Guardians

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese Title** | きせきの結晶 — *Miracle Crystal* |
| **English Release** | August 30, 2006 |
| **Japanese Release** | March 10, 2006 |

### English Blurb

*ONLY THE BEST SHALL PASS THE CRYSTAL GUARDIANS. A remote island... mysterious crystals... and incredible Pokémon just waiting for you to discover them! EX Crystal Guardians takes you to distant shores where more new Pokémon Delta Species, Pokémon-ex, and Pokémon ☆ live among astonishing crystals that can change how Trainers do battle.*

### Setting

The expansion was set on a mysterious island, far from the city of Holon, where Pokémon, both regular and Delta Species, live in harmony. The island also bears naturally growing crystals, which were said to grant special powers if used correctly. **This is the last expansion to feature dual-type Pokémon δ Delta Species.**

### What the Unnamed Island Looks Like

The island is tropical and volcanic, featuring rocky coastlines and sand beaches lined with palm trees, and is the largest island in an archipelago. The coastline contains a number of rocky arches and isolated stacks, and one card depicts what appears to be an area of algae-covered rocks. The surrounding ocean is shown to have mangroves growing near the shore and a coral reef in deeper waters. The inland areas are composed of dense forest and rocky mountains, and multiple cards depict what is either a series of above ground caves or the opening to a subterranean cave system. The forest is jungle-like, containing similar foliage to Mirage Forest, as well as a number of tall, thin coniferous trees.

### The Crystals

Most notably, all parts of the island are covered by glowing crystals. These crystals vary in color, with pink and pale blue crystals the most abundant, and are prevalent in rocky areas and underground. The island suffers from windstorms and features a large number of volcanoes, some of which are visibly smoking. The only known human presence is a castaway sailor, and there are no artificial structures visible on the island.

### Holon Circle

A circular crater containing three tall pink-purple crystals in a triangle formation.

### Delta Behavior on the Unnamed Island

δ Delta Species Pokémon are least prevalent here, with roughly a quarter of evolutionary families containing a δ Delta Species Pokémon. Unusually, δ Delta Species Pokémon here typically don't change their base type until their final Evolution; as a result, only a sixth of individual species are δ Delta Species Pokémon.

### Proximity to Holon

The proximity of the locations to Holon is unclear, though the presence of δ Delta Species Pokémon suggests they may be close by.

### Notable Cards

This set introduces the Kanto and Hoenn starters as both regular and (in some cases) Delta forms. Charizard δ (Lightning/Metal) is the headline Delta card. The Castaway trainer card references the only known human on the island. Crystal Beach and Holon Circle Stadium cards are introduced. The Mysterious Shard and Crystal Shard are Pokémon Tool cards representing the island's crystals. The Gold Stars are Alakazam ☆ and Celebi ☆.

## 6. Set 5 (Story Order) — EX Dragon Frontiers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Japanese Title** | さいはての攻防 — *Offense and Defense of the Furthest Ends* |
| **English Release** | November 8, 2006 |
| **Japanese Release** | June 29, 2006 |

### English Blurb

*Beyond the Horizon Lie the Dragon Frontiers. Journey further than any Trainer has ever been when you explore the islands where powerful Dragon Pokémon rule! In EX Dragon Frontiers, you'll find rare Pokémon-ex Delta Species, long-awaited Pokémon ☆, and new ways to do battle like you've never seen before. Can you hear that distant roar? The Dragon Frontiers are calling you!*

### Setting

The expansion was set on an island far away where natural Delta Species Pokémon thrive.

### What the Dragon Frontiers Look Like

The Dragon Frontiers are described as "beyond the horizon" and referred to as "the islands where powerful Dragon Pokémon rule." Card art clarifies that there are only two islands: one a rocky island in the ocean, the other a mountainous island floating in the sky, surrounded by a ring of clouds. None of the included cards seem to feature locations on top of the floating island, with nearly all either visibly close to the water, below the clouds, or directly showing the island in the background.

### The Lower Island's Terrain

The lower island has little plant life and few beaches, instead featuring rocky coastline. Further inland is a valley of staggered red cliffs and pillar rock formations reminiscent of Sedona, Arizona, sparsely covered in small shrub-like trees. Between this arid area and the ocean is a small grassy area containing a forest of pine trees. More rarely seen is a swamp on the bank of an area of inland water, such as a lake, river, or inlet. The island shows little evidence of human habitation, with the only known human inhabitant an old hermit.

### Ancient Marks Connecting Back to Mirage Forest

The only artificial structures are diamond carvings similar to those seen in Mirage Forest, a piece of unnaturally square cut stone half submerged in the sea, and a stone circle.

### Holon Legacy

A small stone circle composed of three tall standing stones in a triangle formation around a short central stone. The structure is clearly not a natural rock formation, and the Japanese title *Holon's Holy Site* describes it as a holy site, with the specific term used implying a sense of age. This card features the only depiction of a beach in the entire set, though another card also shows palm fronds in the background.

### Delta Behavior at Dragon Frontiers

Nearly all Pokémon in the Dragon Frontiers are δ Delta Species, with all fully evolved Pokémon being δ Delta Species. Additionally, none of those fully evolved δ Delta Species gain the secondary Metal type.

### Why There Is No Metal Type Here

EX Dragon Frontiers is set on an island far away where natural Delta Species Pokémon thrive. Fully-evolved Delta Species Pokémon remain purely one type, as opposed to being partially Metal-type. This was likely due to their artificial creation as a result of the electromagnetic radiation from Holon Tower. Of the 83 Pokémon in the set, only four are not Delta Species.

### Notable Cards

This is the **final Delta Species set.** The Johto starters (Chikorita, Cyndaquil, Totodile lines) all appear as Delta Species, as do the Nidoran lines, the Mareep/Ampharos line, Snorlax δ, and many others. The headline Gold Stars are **Charizard ☆ δ** (Darkness type) and **Mew ☆ δ** (Water type) — both Pokémon having appeared throughout the entire Holon arc. Mew, the Pokémon researchers came to Mirage Forest to find at the very beginning of the story, ends the arc as a Delta Species at the furthest point from Holon. The Island Hermit trainer card references the only known human resident. Holon Legacy is the Stadium card. Holon Energy FF, GL, and WP all return.

### Trivia

The appearance and lore of Mirage Forest and the Dragon Frontiers share some similarities with the movie Jurassic Park.

## 7. The Holon Stadium Cards — What the Source Actually Says

Each of the TCG sets featuring Delta Species include a Stadium card whose name begins with Holon and which depicts a structure with 3 nodes around a central point, appearing from above like the symbol used on Metal Energy cards. It is unclear what these structures mean, though considering that Holon Research Tower is built on the power spot and is apparently the cause of the local δ Delta Species, it is possible that the symbol represents the location of power spots, with the others existing in the other locations acting as the source of the local Delta Species.

On the other hand, use of the word Holon on the cards Holon Circle, Holon Legacy, and Ampharos δ seems to suggest that all locations and their Delta Species do hold some relation to Holon. Additionally, Holon Circle may not actually belong in this category, in which case the pattern of these structures appearing in all locations with Delta Species would not hold. Holon Circle's structure lacks a clear central point and the card's Japanese title *Holon Circle* doesn't perfectly follow the naming scheme, as the other structure cards use the possessive *Holon's* in their titles instead.

> **Source note:** The Bulbapedia Holon page presents two possible interpretations — separate power spots across all locations, or all structures simply referencing back to the Holon Research Tower — and explicitly states neither is confirmed.

## 8. Delta Distribution — How It Changes Across All Locations

Holon, the unnamed island, and the Dragon Frontiers are all outliers in some category. Holon features δ Delta Species Pokémon which only gain a dual Metal-type without changing base type. The unnamed island primarily features δ Delta Species Pokémon which only change base type and become δ Delta Species at their final Evolution. Lastly, the Dragon Frontiers features no dual Metal-types at all, with all δ Delta Species Pokémon only changing their base types. Additionally, all Pokémon in Holon that change base type will be changed to Fire or Lightning type.

| Location | Set | Total δ Families | % Delta | Dual Metal | Base Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Holon | EX Delta Species | 20 / 40 | 50% | 100% of Delta | 55% |
| Holon Lake | EX Holon Phantoms | 23 / 24 | 96% | 39% of Delta | 100% |
| Unnamed Island | EX Crystal Guardians | 10 / 39 | 26% | 40% of Delta | 100% |
| Dragon Frontiers | EX Dragon Frontiers | 42 / 42 | 100% | 0% | 100% |

Pokémon ☆ and non-Legendary/Mythical single stage Pokémon are never δ Delta Species in Holon and the unnamed island, and are always δ Delta Species or Holon's Pokémon in Holon Lake and the Dragon Frontiers. These Pokémon are also never dual Metal-type, though their base type can still be changed to Metal.
