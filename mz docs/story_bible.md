# Pokémon Holon — Story & Lore Bible

---

## Overview

A Pokémon ROM hack built on **pokeemerald expansion** (Pokémon Emerald decomp), set in the **Holon region** from the TCG Delta Species arc. The game tells the story of a scientific accident, an ancient mystery, and a living organism from space that has been dormant beneath a research tower for thousands of years.

The player travels outward from Mirage Forest through five locations that mirror the five TCG sets of the Delta Species arc, piecing together what happened and what it means.

---

## The World

### Holon

A region built around a place — the power spot deep in Mirage Forest. Holon is named after the philosophical concept of a "holon": something that is simultaneously a whole and a part of something larger. The researchers who named it understood they were working with something that connected outward to forces they couldn't fully see.

The region spans five distinct locations, each corresponding to a phase of the Delta phenomenon's spread:

| Location | TCG Set | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mirage Forest | EX Legend Maker | Where it began; crash site; Holon is built here |
| Holon (Research Tower) | EX Delta Species | The accident happens here |
| Holon Lake | EX Holon Phantoms | South of Holon; anomalous Delta patterns |
| Unnamed Island | EX Crystal Guardians | Remote; Delta least prevalent here |
| Dragon Frontiers | EX Dragon Frontiers | Furthest out; natural Delta; floating island above |

### The Power Spot

Deep in Mirage Forest. Radiates mysterious healing energy. Holon was built on top of it. Before the researchers arrived, the ancient civilization treated it as sacred ground — built around it, documented it, never disturbed it.

**What it actually is:** The meteorite crash site. The residual extraterrestrial biological energy of the dormant Deoxys, leaking upward through centuries of rock and earth, is what manifests as the power spot's healing properties. Nobody in the current era knows this. The researchers studied the energy for years without identifying its source.

### The Holon Research Tower

Built by the research team as an electromagnetic tracking instrument, specifically designed to locate Mew by detecting the unique EM signatures associated with Mew sightings in Mirage Forest lore. The Tower sits directly above the buried crash site — and the dormant Deoxys — without the researchers ever realizing it.

The Tower's interior has an unusual organic quality that doesn't match its exterior architecture. Glowing circular machinery. Features that seem less constructed than grown. Whether this is a design choice by the original builders, or something that happened gradually due to proximity to Deoxys, is left open.

## Deoxys

### What Deoxys Is

A mutation of an extraterrestrial virus, exposed to a laser beam in space. Its name derives from DNA. It is not a Pokémon in the conventional sense — it evolved along an entirely different branch of life, one that has no evolutionary relatives anywhere on Earth.

Key properties:
- **While inactive, takes the form of a triangular stone tablet.** This is confirmed canon and central to the story.
- Communicates and perceives the world through aurora electromagnetic wavelengths.
- Disrupted and effectively blinded by EM interference from human technology.
- Can rewrite its own DNA in real time, cycling between four distinct formes — each with different stats, abilities, and behavioral tendencies.
- Can regenerate its entire body from its crystal brain alone.
- Can generate clones — duller in color, no crystal, locked in Normal Forme.
- Possibly a species rather than an individual. Multiple meteorites have been documented; multiple Deoxys are implied.

### Deoxys's Four Formes

Forme-shifting is not a slow process or a conscious one in the way a human makes decisions. It is biological — closer to how a cuttlefish changes color than how a person changes clothes. Each forme represents a different optimization of the same underlying organism:

| Forme | Optimization | Behavioral tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | Balanced | Default state; most social, most communicative |
| Attack | Offense | Aggressive, reactive, threat-focused |
| Defense | Endurance | Closed off, static, oriented toward survival |
| Speed | Mobility | Erratic, evasive, in constant motion |

In a healthy Deoxys, these formes cycle deliberately in response to circumstances. In a fractured one, each fragment is locked into a single optimization with no ability to shift — the behavior becomes exaggerated, fixed, without the balance the other formes provided.

### Deoxys and Rayquaza

Rayquaza attacks Deoxys on sight as a territorial invader — a canonical rivalry. Rayquaza patrols the upper atmosphere and treats anything descending from space as a threat. This isn't malice. It's territory.

In the context of this game: Rayquaza previously encountered whole, coherent Deoxys. What it encounters now are four fractured Delta versions doing things Deoxys has never done — spreading a phenomenon through its territory, locked in behavioral extremes, not responding the way Deoxys should respond. Rayquaza cannot identify what these things are. Its response is aggressive and causes collateral damage.

## The Ancient Civilization

The people who were here before the researchers. They found the power spot, recognized it as something profound, and built their world carefully around it rather than through it.

Their legacy survives in:
- **Diamond carvings** — found in both Mirage Forest and at Dragon Frontiers, separated by ocean. These are records of what they witnessed. They document both Deoxys's arrival and Mew's response to it.
- **Stone domes at Holon Lake** — geometric carvings, great age, trees growing up through the openings. Still structurally sound.
- **The Holon Legacy at Dragon Frontiers** — a stone circle; the Japanese TCG name translates directly as "Holon's Holy Site." Marks a location of significance across an ocean from where Holon was eventually built.
- **Holon Ruins** — an abandoned building in Holon's architectural style with no canonical explanation for what it was. The researchers know about it but haven't been able to date it or explain why it looks the way it does.

The ancient civilization knew something was at the power spot. They didn't try to extract it, replicate it, or use it. Their sites are still standing.

## Mew

Thousands of years ago — long before any researcher arrived — Mew came to the crash site.

As the genetic ancestor of all Pokémon life on Earth, Mew was drawn to Deoxys: a form of life with no connection whatsoever to the tree Mew anchors. Something completely outside its experience. The ancient civilization witnessed this. They documented it. The diamond carvings show both Mew and Deoxys at the same location.

Mew is **not present in the game as a catchable encounter.** It is a historical fact — something the player uncovers gradually through the ancient records. The researchers spent their entire careers searching for Mew in Mirage Forest because of these legends. They built the Tower specifically to find it. They were standing on the answer the whole time: Mew was here, thousands of years ago, drawn to the thing buried beneath their feet.

Mew ☆ δ exists in the TCG as a Water-type Delta at Dragon Frontiers — the final card of the final set, furthest from Holon. What this means in-game is deliberately left open.

## The Accident — Full Sequence

### Before

The Holon Research Tower is broadcasting at full power. The team is running a long-range EM sweep, trying to detect the signature frequencies associated with Mew. The buried stone tablet — Deoxys in its dormant form — has been there for thousands of years. The power spot energy has been radiating upward through it the entire time the Tower has been running. Nothing seemed wrong.

### The Surge

The Tower's signal hits a resonant frequency — not intentionally, not predictably, but inevitably given how long and how hard they were broadcasting. The frequency aligns with Deoxys's natural EM wavelength: the band it uses to communicate, perceive, and exist in the world.

Deoxys responds. The dormant stone tablet activates.

But the Tower's signal is not Deoxys's natural EM frequency. It is a human approximation — powerful but crude. The signal that reaches Deoxys is enough to trigger awakening but not enough to do it cleanly. Deoxys's forme-cycling mechanism — the real-time DNA rewriting that shifts it between formes — activates involuntarily under the unnatural stimulus.

The Delta phenomenon, which has been radiating passively from Deoxys into the power spot for thousands of years, surges outward as the stone tablet cracks. Each forme that cycles through the activation absorbs a wave of the Delta energy as it fragments.

### The Fracture

The stone tablet cracks. Four Delta formes escape.

The **original stone** — the meteorite and the physical vessel — remains. It is still buried beneath the Holon Research Tower. Cracked, dormant again, but present.

The four fragments scatter. Each one is a complete organism, biologically functional, but locked into a single behavioral optimization with no ability to shift. None of them is Deoxys. Each of them is part of what Deoxys was.

### After

The Delta phenomenon spreads outward from Holon. The researchers initially don't understand what happened. Their instruments are reading EM activity unlike anything they've recorded. Pokémon in the surrounding area start presenting Delta mutations. The four Deoxys fragments are moving through the region, each radiating its own version of the phenomenon as it goes.

Rayquaza descends.

## The Four Delta Deoxys Fragments

The Delta spread follows the paths of the four fragments. The further a fragment travels, the further the phenomenon reaches.

### Normal δ — Colorless

Deoxys's baseline. The most communicative forme, the most oriented toward contact. In fracture, this reads as disorientation — searching for the other formes, unable to find them, unable to understand what it is now. Wandering near Holon. Clearly anomalous, not threatening. The first fragment the player encounters, before they know what they're looking at.

### Defense δ — Metal

Deoxys's survival instinct. In fracture, this manifests as total stasis — the fragment has barely moved from the crash site area. Closed off. Static. Anchored by an imperative to endure that has no outlet and no counterbalance from the other formes. The hardest one to approach.

### Attack δ — Darkness

Deoxys's threat response. In fracture, this manifests as aggression without context — reactive, dangerous, fixated on perceived threats. This is the fragment causing most of the collateral damage Rayquaza is responding to. Found somewhere contested and dangerous.

### Speed δ — Lightning

Deoxys's flight response. In fracture, this manifests as pure erratic movement — the fragment has traveled furthest from origin. It is somewhere in Dragon Frontiers, possibly on the floating island. The one that ran. The last one found.

## Delta Species — Rules and Distribution

Delta Species is the phenomenon of Pokémon presenting different elemental types than their species normally would. It is not random — it follows geographic and proximity-based rules tied to the fragments' influence.

| Location | Delta Rules |
|---|---|
| Holon | Dual Metal secondary type at final evolution; base type changes to Fire, Lightning, or pure Metal |
| Holon Lake | Nearly all Pokémon are Delta; anomalous patterns (expected rules break down — Meowth δ is dual Metal unevolved, Magikarp δ starts Metal) |
| Unnamed Island | Least prevalent Delta; changes only at final evolution; last location with dual Metal |
| Dragon Frontiers | 100% Delta at final evolution; NO dual Metal — natural Delta, not Tower-caused |

Dragon Frontiers is the key outlier. The Speed δ fragment has been there long enough that the phenomenon has stabilized into something organic rather than artificial. Delta there isn't a mutation from the Tower — it's become the natural state.

### Holon Stadium Cards (In-Game Equivalent)

In the TCG, each set has a Stadium card named "Holon [X]" showing a 3-node structure resembling the Metal Energy symbol from above. The canonical meaning is unclear — possibly multiple power spots, possibly all referencing the Tower from different angles. This ambiguity is preserved in-game. The 3-node structure may inform the region's geography.

## Themes (Emerging, Not Prescriptive)

These are thematic currents present in the material — not a message the game is trying to deliver, just patterns that emerge from what happens. Other themes will develop as the story and characters are built out.

**Knowledge without understanding.** The researchers found the right place and built the wrong thing. They had data about Mew, data about the power spot, data about the EM anomalies — and assembled it into a conclusion that caused an accident. The ancient civilization had less information and left less damage.

**What dormancy means.** Deoxys wasn't gone — it was waiting. The power spot wasn't mysterious energy — it was biology. The stone ruins weren't decorations — they were records. The game is full of things that look inert and aren't.

**Transformation vs. loss.** Every Pokémon affected by Delta is changed. Whether that's loss depends on what you think they were. The fragments of Deoxys are changed, locked into single formes. Whether that's tragedy depends on whether you think they can be made whole again.

**The limits of territory.** Rayquaza has a claim on the sky. That claim made sense before four fractured alien organisms started moving through it doing things Deoxys has never done. Territorial logic applied to something genuinely new produces results that feel wrong even when the logic is sound.

## Open Questions (Pending Decisions)

The following are not yet decided and should be addressed in subsequent design sessions:

- **Region geography and map** — exact layout of the five locations, travel between them
- **Progression structure** — gyms vs. investigation/expedition framing vs. hybrid
- **Player character** — identity, backstory, reason for being in Mirage Forest
- **Starter Pokémon** — three Delta Species; not yet chosen
- **Key NPCs** — lead researcher, rival or equivalent, supporting cast
- **Delta Deoxys encounter design** — mechanical approach (fixed forme / cycling mid-battle / forme-at-catch determines permanent stats)
- **Deoxys reformation** — whether it can reform, whether the game resolves it or leaves it open
- **Rayquaza encounter** — structure, trigger, outcome
- **The floating island** — what is on its surface; when is it accessible
- **Post-game content**
